Plan your destination

Compare the essentials before applying.

Use this planning checklist when comparing countries. The requirements below are considerations to research, not a statement that any visa, job or sponsorship route is available to every applicant.

Planning area What to check Where to verify
Job eligibility Qualifications, experience, skills, age criteria where applicable, language and professional registration. Official vacancy notice and employer's recruitment team.
Work authorisation Visa category, sponsorship requirements, work conditions, permitted occupation and duration. Destination country's official immigration authority.
Salary and benefits Gross versus net pay, currency, deductions, overtime, insurance, leave and other benefits. Written employment contract and relevant labour authority.
Relocation costs Travel, accommodation, deposits, document certification, insurance and settlement expenses. Written estimates and independent official guidance.
Recruitment legitimacy Employer identity, recruiter authorisation where required, contact details and payment requests. Official employer channels and relevant government registries.
Qualification recognition Credential equivalency, licensing examinations, translations and skills assessments. Destination-country regulator or recognised assessment authority.
🛡️

Make informed decisions about overseas employment.

Never rely solely on social-media posts, informal messages or verbal promises. Verify the employer, recruitment agency where applicable, employment contract and visa route independently. Be especially cautious of guaranteed-visa or guaranteed-job claims linked to urgent payments.

Your international application roadmap

Prepare methodically for your next move.

International recruitment can require more preparation than a domestic application. Use this sequence to organise your research and reduce avoidable mistakes.

01

Choose your target

Identify suitable countries, occupations, salary expectations and relocation considerations based on your profile.

02

Verify the vacancy

Confirm that the role exists, the employer is legitimate and the advertised requirements match your qualifications.

03

Prepare your documents

Organise your CV, education records, references, certificates and translations or assessments when required.

04

Check work permission

Confirm the correct visa or work-permit process and review the written contract before making relocation commitments.

Application readiness

Organise your professional documents.

Prepare documents only as required by the verified employer or official application process. Do not send sensitive identity or financial documents to an unverified person or website.

Quick answers

International jobs FAQs.

Answers to common questions about overseas employment, work authorisation, documents and safe job searching.

This page provides international career resources and destination research links. It does not confirm active vacancies, employer sponsorship or a particular hiring opportunity. Verify current openings directly with the employer or official recruitment source.

It depends on the destination, occupation, employer and immigration rules. Some professions require recognised qualifications, registration, licensing, language tests or formal skills assessments. Confirm the requirements with the relevant official authority.

Sponsorship depends on the employer, job, destination-country rules and your eligibility. Do not assume that an employer offers sponsorship unless it is explicitly confirmed and independently verified. Review the official immigration requirements before accepting an offer.

Depending on the role and country, an application may require a CV, education records, employment references, professional licences, language evidence or other documents. Immigration applications may have separate requirements. Follow the official instructions for your specific case.

Independently verify the employer and recruiter, check official contact details, read the written contract and be suspicious of guaranteed jobs or visas, pressure to pay immediately, unverifiable email addresses and requests for passwords or one-time passcodes. Never rely on a recruiter-provided link alone to verify legitimacy.

No. Career resources and recruitment-related enquiries do not guarantee an interview, employment, visa approval, sponsorship or salary outcome. Hiring decisions are made by employers, and immigration decisions are made by the relevant government authorities.

Use the official immigration or government website of the destination country. Review the visa category, eligibility, permitted work, application procedure, costs and any sponsorship requirements. Seek qualified professional advice when your circumstances require it.

## 1. Purpose

The `/assets/documents/policies/` directory organizes approved policy documents published or referenced by the MedOrbits website.

It provides a consistent location for public-facing policies, user notices, service terms, disclaimers, and other documents that explain how MedOrbits operates and how users should interact with its services.

Policy documents must be accurate, clearly written, appropriately authorized, accessible, and maintained according to applicable legal and operational requirements.

**Important:** The directory structure and filenames in this README are recommendations. They do not confirm that the example policy documents already exist or have been legally reviewed.

—

## 3. Scope

Policies in this directory may relate to:

- Website usage and user responsibilities.
- Privacy and personal-data processing.
- Application preparation and document submission.
- Educational and admission guidance.
- Healthcare information and educational resources.
- Career guidance and recruitment services.
- Paid services, refunds, and cancellations.
- Cookies and website technologies.
- Accessibility and user support.
- Document retention and information security.

Only publish policies relevant to services that MedOrbits actually provides.

—

## 4. Policy Accuracy and Approval

A policy must describe actual practices and approved business rules.

Before publication:

1. Confirm the policy’s purpose and scope.
2. Verify the operational practices it describes.
3. Check applicable legal and regulatory requirements.
4. Obtain appropriate internal or professional review.
5. Confirm that links, contact details, and referenced procedures work.
6. Assign a version and effective date where appropriate.
7. Publish the approved version.
8. Record the review and publication status.

Do not invent legal obligations, contractual terms, business commitments, or regulatory approvals.

Policies with legal, privacy, financial, healthcare, or employment implications should receive appropriate qualified review before publication.

—

## 5. Writing and Formatting Standards

Policy documents should be clear, precise, and easy to navigate.

### Recommended structure

Where appropriate, include:

1. Policy title.
2. Purpose.
3. Scope.
4. Definitions.
5. Policy requirements.
6. Roles and responsibilities.
7. Procedures or user instructions.
8. Exceptions and limitations.
9. Contact or support information.
10. Effective date and revision history.

Not every policy needs every section. Adapt the structure to the document’s purpose.

### Writing guidelines

- Use plain language wherever possible.
- Define specialized terminology.
- Separate requirements from recommendations.
- Use headings that accurately describe each section.
- Avoid contradictory statements.
- Identify relevant limitations and exceptions.
- Use consistent terminology across policies.
- Distinguish current requirements from future plans.
- Avoid guarantees that the organization cannot substantiate.

—

## 6. Privacy and Personal Data

Privacy-related policies must reflect the actual personal-data practices of MedOrbits.

Where applicable, document:

- Categories of personal data collected.
- Purposes and applicable legal bases for processing.
- Collection methods and relevant user interactions.
- Data sharing and service-provider involvement.
- Retention and deletion practices.
- Security safeguards.
- User rights and available request procedures.
- Contact details for privacy-related enquiries.
- Applicable requirements for children or other protected users.

Do not claim that data is encrypted, never shared, automatically deleted, or stored in a particular location unless the statement has been verified.

Do not include real applicant records, identity documents, medical records, credentials, or other confidential information in public policy files.

—

## 7. Terms of Service and Disclaimers

Terms and disclaimers must accurately describe the relationship between MedOrbits and its users.

For education, admissions, healthcare information, and career services:

- Distinguish informational guidance from official decisions.
- Do not imply affiliation with a government authority or institution without authorization.
- Do not guarantee admission, employment, eligibility, or financial outcomes.
- Identify important service limitations.
- Explain user responsibilities where relevant.
- Ensure that terms are consistent with the services actually offered.
- Obtain appropriate legal review before relying on contractual or liability-related language.

A disclaimer does not automatically remove legal responsibilities or override applicable law.

—

## 8. Refunds, Cancellations, and Paid Services

If MedOrbits offers paid services, any published refund or cancellation policy must reflect the actual approved commercial terms.

Where relevant, clarify:

- Which services are covered.
- Applicable fees and payment conditions.
- Cancellation procedures.
- Refund eligibility and exclusions.
- Relevant time limits.
- Expected processing procedures.
- Contact methods for payment disputes.
- Any mandatory rights under applicable law.

Do not publish example refund periods, non-refundable conditions, or payment guarantees as actual terms unless they have been approved and verified.

—

## 9. Educational, Healthcare, and Recruitment Policies

Policies associated with education, healthcare information, and recruitment must be especially careful about accuracy and user expectations.

### Educational and admissions information

- Verify current requirements against the relevant official authority.
- Identify the applicable academic year where needed.
- Distinguish MedOrbits guidance from official institutional decisions.
- Avoid unsupported claims about eligibility, fees, deadlines, or admission outcomes.

### Healthcare information

- Distinguish general educational information from individualized medical advice.
- Avoid unsupported treatment claims or guarantees.
- Identify appropriate limitations where necessary.
- Obtain relevant review for medical or clinical claims.

### Recruitment and career services

- Describe actual services and limitations.
- Avoid guaranteeing employment or selection.
- Handle candidate information appropriately.
- Use fair and relevant recruitment procedures.
- Follow applicable employment and privacy requirements.

—

## 10. Document Submission and Retention

Policies concerning document submission must describe the actual submission channels and handling arrangements.

Where applicable, explain:

- Which documents are requested and why.
- Accepted file formats and size limits.
- How documents should be submitted.
- Who may access submitted information.
- How documents are stored and protected.
- Applicable retention and deletion procedures.
- How users can request help or report a problem.

**Security requirement:** Never place completed application forms, passports, identity documents, medical records, or other private user uploads in a publicly accessible policy directory.

Use appropriately secured storage for sensitive records.

—

## 11. Policy Versions and Review Register

Maintain a record of policy status and revisions.

A review register may use the following fields:

| Field | Description |
|—|—|
| Policy ID | Stable identifier for the policy |
| Policy title | Name of the document |
| File path | Repository location |
| Version | Current document version |
| Owner | Responsible role or team |
| Effective date | Date the policy takes effect |
| Last reviewed | Date of the most recent review |
| Next review | Planned review date, where applicable |
| Status | Draft, under review, approved, published, or retired |
| Approval record | Reference to the applicable approval |

Example structure:

```text
policy-review-register.md
```

Do not mark a policy as approved or legally reviewed unless that status is supported by an actual review record.

## 12. Publication and Website Integration

Policies may be published as Markdown, HTML, or PDF depending on the website’s architecture.

Use clear, accessible links.

Example:

```html
<a href=“/assets/documents/policies/disclaimer.md”>
  Read the disclaimer
</a>
```

This example is appropriate only if the Markdown file is intended to be served directly and the hosting configuration supports it. A rendered HTML policy page is often more suitable for public website navigation.

### Integration rules

- Confirm the target file exists.
- Prefer readable web pages for important policies.
- Provide downloadable PDF versions where useful.
- Use descriptive link text.
- Ensure links work on mobile and desktop.
- Keep the footer and relevant forms consistent with the current published policies.
- Update all links when files move.
- Do not expose private operational documents through public routes.

—

## 13. Accessibility

Policy documents must be accessible to their intended audience.

- Use semantic headings and logical reading order.
- Maintain sufficient text contrast.
- Avoid excessively small text.
- Use descriptive links.
- Provide accessible alternatives where practical.
- Ensure tables remain readable on small screens.
- Avoid relying on colour alone to communicate status.
- Use accessible PDF structure when distributing PDFs.
- Test keyboard navigation and browser zoom.
- Support required language versions where available.

If a translated policy is provided, ensure that it remains consistent with the approved source and clearly identify the applicable version.

—

## 14. Copyright and Third-Party Material

Before publishing policy content:

- Confirm ownership or permission to reproduce third-party material.
- Retain required attribution and copyright notices.
- Verify the source of referenced documents.
- Do not alter official documents in a way that misrepresents their meaning.
- Identify external sources where appropriate.
- Review embedded images, fonts, and other assets for licensing restrictions.

Do not assume that a document is freely reusable because it is publicly available online.

—

## 15. Security and Confidentiality

The policies directory should contain documents approved for repository storage and intended for their designated audience.

- Do not store passwords, access tokens, or credentials.
- Do not include personal applicant records.
- Do not publish confidential internal procedures without authorization.
- Review document metadata and embedded content before publication.
- Use access-controlled storage for restricted policy materials.
- Keep public policy pages separate from confidential operational records.
- Follow applicable privacy, security, and retention requirements.

Publicly served documents should be treated as accessible to anyone who can reach their URLs.

—

## 16. Maintenance Workflow

When creating or revising a policy:

1. Identify the reason for the change.
2. Confirm the responsible policy owner.
3. Review actual business practices and applicable requirements.
4. Draft the revised content.
5. Obtain required internal or professional review.
6. Check links, dates, terminology, and contact details.
7. Assign the appropriate version and effective date.
8. Publish the approved document.
9. Update the review register and website links.
10. Retire superseded versions appropriately.
11. Check any forms or workflows that refer to the policy.
12. Record the change in the document’s revision history.

Review policies when relevant services, business practices, legal requirements, or user-facing workflows change.

—

## 17. Pre-Publication Checklist

Before publishing a policy:

- [ ] The policy describes actual approved practices.
- [ ] The document has an appropriate owner.
- [ ] Legal or professional review has been obtained where necessary.
- [ ] Dates, version numbers, and contact details are correct.
- [ ] External references and links have been verified.
- [ ] No unsupported guarantees or legal claims are included.
- [ ] Privacy and security statements reflect actual practices.
- [ ] Copyright and licensing requirements are satisfied.
- [ ] Accessibility has been checked.
- [ ] No confidential information is exposed.
- [ ] The publication path is correct.
- [ ] Related website pages and forms have been updated.
- [ ] The review register reflects the actual approval status.

—

## 18. Troubleshooting

### Policy link does not work

Check the file path, filename capitalization, deployment output, and routing configuration.

### Published policy is outdated

Review the relevant requirements, obtain the necessary approval, publish the updated version, and remove or clearly label superseded content.

### Policy contradicts website content

Review related pages, forms, checkout flows, and footer links. Resolve the inconsistency before continuing publication.

### Policy makes an unsupported legal claim

Remove or correct the claim and obtain appropriate qualified review before republishing.

### Confidential information is discovered

Remove public access promptly, investigate the exposure, and follow the applicable privacy incident procedure.

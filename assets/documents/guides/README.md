## 1. Purpose

The `guides/` directory organises instructional documents that help students, applicants, job seekers, website visitors, and other users understand MedOrbits services and complete common tasks.

Guides should provide clear, practical, accurate, and accessible instructions. They should explain what users need to do, what documents or information may be required, where to find authoritative information, and what steps to take when a problem occurs.

This directory may contain guides covering:

- Educational admissions and counselling.
- Entrance examinations and application preparation.
- Medical, dental, and allied healthcare course admissions.
- Document preparation and verification.
- Scholarships, fees, and financial planning.
- Careers, job applications, and recruitment services.
- Website navigation and account assistance.
- Forms, document uploads, and PDF usage.
- Privacy, security, and responsible handling of personal information.
- Frequently asked questions and troubleshooting.

**Important:** Guides must distinguish MedOrbits’ own services from official government processes, examination authorities, counselling bodies, colleges, employers, and other third-party organisations.

## 4. Guide Writing Standards

Every guide should be written for its intended audience and should prioritise clear actions over unnecessary technical language.

### Recommended structure

1. **Title:** Clearly identify the task or subject.
2. **Purpose:** Explain what the guide helps the reader accomplish.
3. **Audience:** Identify who should use the guide.
4. **Before you begin:** List prerequisites, eligibility checks, or required information.
5. **Step-by-step instructions:** Present actions in the order they should be completed.
6. **Required documents:** List applicable documents, file formats, or supporting evidence.
7. **Official references:** Link to relevant authoritative sources.
8. **Common mistakes:** Explain avoidable errors and how to prevent them.
9. **Troubleshooting:** Provide corrective actions for common problems.
10. **Support and escalation:** Explain how users can seek assistance.
11. **Important notices:** Include relevant deadlines, fees, limitations, or disclaimers.
12. **Revision details:** Record the version and latest review date.

Not every guide requires every section. Keep the structure proportionate to the task.

### Writing rules

- Use plain, professional English.
- Use headings that describe the content below them.
- Prefer numbered steps for sequential tasks.
- Use checklists for documents and prerequisites.
- Expand abbreviations at first use.
- Explain technical terms when readers may not know them.
- Use consistent terminology across the website.
- Avoid unsupported promises or guaranteed outcomes.
- Clearly identify mandatory requirements versus recommendations.
- Avoid presenting estimates as confirmed official figures.
- Provide meaningful link text instead of phrases such as “click here.”
- Make instructions usable on mobile devices and assistive technologies.

## 5. Admissions and Education Guides

Education-related guides may cover MBBS, BDS, BAMS, BUMS, nursing, paramedical courses, undergraduate programmes, postgraduate programmes, and other educational pathways supported by MedOrbits.

Depending on the subject, a guide may explain:

- Course duration and academic structure.
- Eligibility and prerequisite qualifications.
- Entrance examinations.
- Application and registration steps.
- Counselling procedures and choice filling.
- Required documents.
- Fee components and payment deadlines.
- Seat-allocation and admission stages.
- College selection and comparison.
- Official notices, correction windows, and important dates.
- Common application mistakes.

### Accuracy requirements

Before publishing admissions information:

1. Identify the relevant academic year and jurisdiction.
2. Check the applicable official examination, counselling, regulator, or institutional source.
3. Verify eligibility criteria, dates, fees, and document requirements.
4. Record the source URL and date checked.
5. Distinguish official requirements from general guidance.
6. Recheck the guide when a new notice or revised schedule is issued.

Do not reuse previous-year dates, eligibility rules, cutoffs, or fee figures without checking whether they remain applicable.

Admission outcomes, seat availability, eligibility decisions, and counselling results are determined by the relevant authorities and institutions, not by the guide itself.

## 6. Entrance Examination Guides

Examination guides should help users understand registration, preparation, examination-day requirements, and result-related processes.

Where applicable, include:

- Official examination name and conducting authority.
- Eligibility requirements.
- Registration process and official portal.
- Application dates and correction windows.
- Examination pattern and syllabus references.
- Required identification and documents.
- Admit-card instructions.
- Examination-day rules.
- Answer-key and result procedures.
- Counselling or next-step information.
- Official helpdesk or grievance process.

Preparation advice must be distinguished from official examination rules. Link to the current official bulletin or information brochure whenever requirements can change.

Never claim that a guide, practice set, or consultancy service guarantees qualification, a rank, a seat, or a job.

## 7. Document Preparation Guides

Document-preparation guides should explain how to prepare files for an application without collecting unnecessary personal information.

Potential topics include:

- Scanning documents clearly.
- Saving documents as PDF.
- Combining multiple pages into one file.
- Compressing oversized files.
- Checking page orientation and readability.
- Naming files consistently.
- Preparing photographs and signatures.
- Checking file-size and format restrictions.
- Reviewing a document before upload.
- Keeping original documents secure.
- Correcting common upload errors.

### Recommended file-naming pattern

```text
document-type_academic-year_version.pdf
```

Example:

```text
marksheet_2026_v1.pdf
```

Use examples only as naming guidance. Users should follow the specific instructions of the institution or portal receiving the application.

### Privacy requirements

- Do not ask users to upload documents unless necessary for the stated task.
- Explain why sensitive documents are required and how they will be handled.
- Never publish identity documents, marksheets, certificates, signatures, or other personal records in public asset directories.
- Use secure, access-controlled storage for private applicant documents.
- Do not include real personal data in sample screenshots or demonstration files.

## 8. Fees and Scholarship Guides

Financial guides should explain the distinction between tuition fees, counselling fees, registration charges, deposits, hostel charges, examination fees, and other applicable costs.

Where relevant, include:

- The institution or authority responsible for the charge.
- The applicable academic year.
- Whether the amount is official, estimated, or indicative.
- Payment methods accepted by the relevant provider.
- Refund or cancellation rules, when verified.
- Scholarship eligibility and application requirements.
- Official scholarship portals.
- Required supporting documents.
- Deadlines and renewal conditions.

Never publish unverified payment instructions or request that users transfer money to unofficial accounts. Do not promise scholarship approval, admission, employment, or a financial return.

If a fee or deadline cannot be verified, clearly mark it as unverified or omit it until a reliable source is available.

## 9. Careers and Recruitment Guides

Career and recruitment guides may help users prepare for employment opportunities and understand the application process.

Possible subjects include:

- CV and résumé preparation.
- Cover letters and professional profiles.
- Job-search techniques.
- Interview preparation.
- Skills development and career planning.
- Job-registration forms.
- Document submission.
- Application-status follow-up.
- Employer communication.
- Recruitment process explanations.
- Candidate privacy and fraud awareness.

### Job information standards

For each specific opportunity, provide verified information where available:

- Employer or recruiting organisation.
- Job title and responsibilities.
- Location and work arrangement.
- Required qualifications and experience.
- Application deadline.
- Official application link or verified contact channel.
- Salary information only when supported by a reliable source.
- Relevant terms, conditions, and limitations.

Do not invent vacancies, employers, salaries, selection criteria, or official partnerships.

Make clear whether MedOrbits is providing general career guidance, application assistance, recruitment support, or an independently verified job listing. Do not imply that registration guarantees an interview, selection, or employment.

### Recruitment safety

Warn users to verify employers and avoid suspicious requests for money, passwords, one-time passcodes, or unnecessary identity information. Explain that users should independently verify any fee or payment request associated with a job opportunity.

## 11. Privacy and Security Guides

Guides involving personal information must support privacy-conscious behaviour.

Explain, where relevant:

- What information a user needs to provide.
- Why the information is required.
- Which fields are optional or mandatory.
- How to avoid uploading unnecessary documents.
- How to recognise suspicious messages and impersonation.
- How to protect passwords and verification codes.
- How to access support through verified channels.
- How to report suspected misuse.

Do not promise that data is encrypted, retained for a particular period, deleted automatically, or shared with no third parties unless the implementation and published privacy policy support those statements.

Guides must remain consistent with the actual MedOrbits privacy policy, data-handling practices, and applicable legal requirements.

## 12. Official Sources and External Links

Whenever a guide contains information that may change, link to an authoritative source.

Prefer:

1. Government departments and official portals.
2. Examination and counselling authorities.
3. Recognised regulators.
4. Official institutional websites.
5. Original employer or service-provider pages.
6. Other reputable sources when official information is unavailable.

For each important source, record:

- Source title.
- Publishing authority.
- URL.
- Date accessed or checked.
- Relevant section or notice.
- Any limitations on interpretation or reuse.

Use HTTPS where available and check links periodically. Avoid using third-party summaries as the sole authority for high-impact admission, financial, healthcare, or employment requirements.

An external link does not mean the source endorses MedOrbits.

## 13. Accessibility and Responsive Design

All digital guides should be readable on phones, tablets, laptops, and desktop screens.

Apply the following practices:

- Use a logical heading hierarchy.
- Ensure text has sufficient contrast against its background.
- Provide meaningful alternative text for informative images.
- Do not use colour as the only way to communicate a status.
- Use descriptive links.
- Give tables clear headers and readable layouts.
- Avoid unnecessarily wide tables on mobile screens.
- Provide captions or transcripts for instructional media where appropriate.
- Ensure documents remain readable when zoomed.
- Use selectable text in PDFs wherever possible.
- Check reading order and document structure.
- Avoid images of text when ordinary text is practical.

For branded MedOrbits documents, use the approved design system while maintaining readable contrast and accessibility. Visual styling must not reduce the legibility of instructions or warnings.

## 14. File Naming and Versioning

Use descriptive, consistent, lowercase filenames.

Recommended examples:

```text
mbbs-admission-guide.md
entrance-exam-registration-guide.md
document-upload-guide.md
scholarship-application-guide.md
job-application-guide.md
website-troubleshooting-guide.md
```

Avoid filenames that contain spaces, unexplained abbreviations, or ambiguous labels such as `new-guide-final-final.md`.

### Versioning recommendations

- Use `1.0.0` for the first approved release of a guide.
- Increment the minor version for meaningful additions that do not invalidate existing instructions.
- Increment the patch version for small corrections and clarifications.
- Use a major version when a substantial change makes the previous instructions incompatible or misleading.

For date-sensitive guides, include the applicable academic year or effective date in the guide itself. A version number alone does not establish that the information is current.

## 16. Review and Approval Workflow

Use a controlled workflow for guide publication.

### Step 1 — Draft

Create the guide using the approved structure and identify its intended audience.

### Step 2 — Source verification

Check factual claims, official requirements, external links, dates, and figures.

### Step 3 — Content review

Check clarity, completeness, terminology, grammar, and consistency with other MedOrbits pages.

### Step 4 — Privacy and rights review

Check personal-data handling, copyright, image permissions, and required attributions.

### Step 5 — Technical review

Verify file paths, hyperlinks, formatting, responsive layout, and accessibility.

### Step 6 — Approval

Assign the appropriate status and publish only when required checks are complete.

### Step 7 — Maintenance

Revisit the guide when official requirements, website interfaces, service procedures, or source material changes.

Recommended status values:

- `draft`
- `in_review`
- `approved`
- `published`
- `needs_update`
- `archived`

Use these labels consistently if the project adopts a structured publishing workflow.

## 17. Troubleshooting

### A guide contains outdated information

Check the latest authoritative source, update the affected sections, and record the review date. If the guide may mislead users, temporarily unpublish it until corrected.

### An external link is broken

Locate the current page on the original authority’s website. Update the link only after verifying that the destination contains the intended information.

### A PDF is difficult to read

Check font embedding, text contrast, page margins, image resolution, text selection, and mobile readability. Regenerate the PDF from the approved source if necessary.

### A guide refers to a missing page

Verify the actual repository path and production URL. Correct the link or remove the reference until the page is available.

### A user cannot complete a process

Check whether the external portal has changed, whether eligibility or deadlines apply, and whether the guide matches the current workflow. Direct the user to the responsible authority for decisions that MedOrbits cannot make.

### A guide contains an unverified claim

Remove or qualify the claim until reliable evidence is available. Do not treat an existing draft or older publication as proof of accuracy.

## 20. Disclaimer

MedOrbits guides are intended to provide general educational, procedural, career, and website-use information. They do not replace official notices, professional advice, institutional decisions, examination rules, or applicable legal requirements.

## 1. Purpose

The `brochures/` directory organises digital brochures, downloadable service guides, course information booklets, and promotional documents published by MedOrbits.

Brochures should communicate services and information clearly, present a consistent brand identity, and help readers understand available options and next steps.

This directory may contain brochures covering:

- MBBS and medical admissions guidance.
- BDS, BAMS, BUMS, nursing, and paramedical courses.
- Undergraduate and postgraduate education pathways.
- Entrance examinations and counselling guidance.
- College and university information.
- Scholarships and education planning.
- Career guidance and job assistance.
- Recruitment services and candidate support.
- Document preparation and application assistance.
- MedOrbits company profile and service overview.
- Frequently requested guidance resources.

**Important:** Brochures must not imply official affiliation, guaranteed admission, guaranteed employment, or endorsement by an institution or authority unless that claim has been explicitly verified and authorised.

## 5. Standard Brochure Content

Where appropriate, each brochure should contain the following sections.

1. **Cover:** Brochure title, brand identity, and publication version.
2. **Introduction:** Explain the topic or service.
3. **Audience:** Identify who may benefit from the information.
4. **Overview:** Summarise the relevant course, service, or process.
5. **Key information:** Present verified facts and practical details.
6. **Process:** Explain the steps a reader may need to take.
7. **Requirements:** List relevant prerequisites or documents.
8. **Official references:** Link to authoritative sources where applicable.
9. **Contact details:** Provide verified support information.
10. **Disclaimer:** Explain limitations and distinguish guidance from official decisions.
11. **Publication details:** Record the version and review date.

Not every brochure needs every section. Keep the content relevant to its intended audience.

## 6. Brand and Visual Design Standards

Brochures should follow the approved MedOrbits visual identity and remain consistent with the website.

### Design principles

- Use a clear typographic hierarchy.
- Keep headings, body text, captions, and footnotes distinguishable.
- Use consistent margins, spacing, and alignment.
- Maintain strong text-to-background contrast.
- Use images that are relevant to the subject.
- Avoid overcrowded layouts and excessive decorative effects.
- Use consistent icons and visual elements.
- Keep contact information easy to find.
- Ensure the design remains legible on mobile screens and in print.

### Recommended visual direction

Where consistent with the approved brand system, use a premium palette built around sapphire blue, deep navy, burgundy, champagne gold, and softly tinted backgrounds.

Avoid pure-white or transparent sections when the established design requires a tinted background. Do not sacrifice readability or accessibility to maintain a particular aesthetic.

### Typography

- Use approved project fonts where their licence permits the intended use.
- Embed fonts in PDFs where appropriate and permitted.
- Use readable body text and clear heading sizes.
- Check font rendering across PDF viewers.
- Avoid long passages in decorative display fonts.

### Images and graphics

- Use original, licensed, or otherwise authorised images.
- Record attribution requirements.
- Do not use official logos, seals, or institutional marks in a misleading way.
- Avoid visual elements that imply an unauthorised partnership or endorsement.
- Provide meaningful alternative text for informative images in digital brochures.

## 7. Accuracy and Source Verification

Brochures containing admissions, examination, college, scholarship, employment, or financial information must be reviewed for factual accuracy.

Before publication:

1. Identify the applicable academic year or effective date.
2. Locate the original official source.
3. Verify eligibility rules, fees, deadlines, and other changing details.
4. Check names, addresses, links, and contact information.
5. Distinguish official requirements from general recommendations.
6. Record source references and the date checked.
7. Remove or qualify unsupported claims.
8. Recheck the brochure when an important source changes.

### Admissions-specific requirements

For medical admissions and other regulated courses, verify information against the applicable official examination authority, counselling body, regulator, institution, or government portal.

Do not reuse old-year cutoffs, counselling schedules, fees, or eligibility requirements without checking their current applicability.

### Financial and career claims

Do not publish unverified scholarship amounts, tuition fees, salaries, placement statistics, or employment guarantees.

Where an amount is estimated, label it clearly and explain its source and applicable period.

## 8. Copyright and Licence Compliance

Before publishing a brochure, check the rights associated with:

- Photographs and illustrations.
- Fonts and icon sets.
- Charts, maps, and diagrams.
- Third-party text and publications.
- Logos and trademarks.
- Templates and design assets.

Record relevant source URLs, copyright notices, licence terms, and attribution requirements.

Do not assume that content is free to reuse because it appears on a public website.

For licence records, refer to:

`/assets/documents/licences/`

Use the original licence or permission terms as the authority for reuse. A brochure’s inclusion in this directory does not grant permission to redistribute third-party material.

## 9. PDF Export and Quality Requirements

Before releasing a brochure as a PDF, verify the exported document.

### Layout

- Page size is correct.
- Margins and alignment are consistent.
- No text or graphics are clipped.
- Page order is correct.
- Images have sufficient resolution.
- Headings and page numbers are positioned consistently.

### Typography and content

- Fonts render correctly.
- Required fonts are embedded where appropriate.
- Text remains readable at normal zoom.
- Links work where included.
- Contact details are correct.
- No placeholder text remains.
- No unintended blank pages are present.
- No outdated dates or figures remain.

### Accessibility

- Text is selectable where possible.
- Reading order is logical.
- Headings and document structure are accessible where supported.
- Informative images have appropriate alternative text in the digital source.
- Colour contrast is sufficient.
- The document remains usable when viewed on a phone.

### File optimisation

- Compress oversized images without making them unreadable.
- Remove unnecessary metadata that exposes private information.
- Avoid excessive file sizes that prevent practical downloading.
- Test the PDF in more than one viewer when possible.

## 10. File Naming Convention

Use descriptive, lowercase filenames with hyphens.

Recommended examples:

```text
medorbits-company-profile.pdf
mbbs-admission-guide-2026.pdf
bds-course-overview.pdf
entrance-exam-guide-2026.pdf
scholarship-guidance.pdf
career-assistance-services.pdf
recruitment-services-overview.pdf
document-preparation-guide.pdf
```

Use an academic year only when the content is specifically tied to that year.

Avoid ambiguous filenames such as:

```text
final.pdf
new-brochure.pdf
final-final-updated.pdf
document2.pdf
```

A file’s name should identify its subject and, where relevant, its publication year or version.

## 11. Versioning and Publication Status

Maintain a version number and review date for brochures that are revised over time.

Recommended versioning:

- `1.0.0` — first approved release.
- `1.1.0` — compatible content additions or improvements.
- `1.0.1` — minor corrections.
- `2.0.0` — major content or structural revision.

Recommended publication statuses:

- `draft`
- `in_review`
- `approved`
- `published`
- `needs_update`
- `archived`

Do not present a draft brochure as approved or publish outdated material without a clear reason and appropriate warning.

For brochures tied to academic years, review and update them when new official schedules, eligibility rules, or fee structures are published.

## 13. Website Integration

Brochures may be linked from course pages, service pages, college profiles, career pages, or a central downloads page.

When integrating brochures:

- Use descriptive link text.
- Display the correct title and file type.
- Show the file size where useful.
- Identify the publication year when relevant.
- Open or download the correct file.
- Check links after deployment.
- Avoid broken paths and obsolete filenames.
- Ensure the brochure is publicly accessible only when intended for public distribution.
- Keep private or personalised documents out of public download directories.

If the website generates a brochure catalogue from JSON, validate that each listed file exists and that its publication status allows public display.

Do not use local development URLs in production links.

## 14. Accessibility and Language Support

Brochures should be accessible to their intended readers.

Where appropriate:

- Use plain language.
- Explain technical abbreviations.
- Use readable body text.
- Maintain sufficient contrast.
- Use logical heading levels.
- Avoid conveying essential information through colour alone.
- Provide accessible digital text alongside image-heavy content.
- Use correctly rendered language-specific characters.
- Review translations for meaning and accuracy.
- Keep translated versions consistent with the approved source.

If brochures are published in English, Hindi, Bengali, Assamese, or other languages, verify the language and terminology before release.

Do not assume that an automated translation is accurate enough for legal, medical, admissions, or financial instructions without appropriate review.

## 15. Review and Approval Workflow

Use a consistent publication workflow.

### Step 1 — Prepare

Define the audience, purpose, scope, and intended distribution channel.

### Step 2 — Draft

Prepare content using the approved design and content standards.

### Step 3 — Verify

Check facts, figures, links, dates, source references, and licence requirements.

### Step 4 — Review

Review language, branding, accessibility, privacy, and any sensitive claims.

### Step 5 — Export

Generate the PDF or web version from the approved source.

### Step 6 — Test

Check the file on mobile and desktop, inspect print layout, and test links.

### Step 7 — Approve

Record the publication status, version, and review date.

### Step 8 — Publish

Upload the approved file and add it to the correct website page or brochure catalogue.

### Step 9 — Maintain

Update or archive the brochure when its information is no longer current.

## 17. Troubleshooting

### The PDF layout differs from the source

Check page size, font availability, embedded fonts, image scaling, export settings, and print margins. Regenerate the PDF from the approved source and inspect it again.

### The brochure is too large to download

Optimise images, remove unnecessary embedded resources, and use suitable PDF compression without compromising text or image quality.

### A download link is broken

Verify the actual repository path, filename, case sensitivity, deployment output, and public URL.

### A brochure contains outdated information

Verify the latest authoritative source, update the content, increment the version, and republish the approved file. Archive the previous version if needed for traceability.

### An image or logo has unclear usage rights

Check the original source and licence. Obtain permission or replace the asset with an authorised alternative if reuse cannot be established.

### A brochure is difficult to read on mobile

Review body text size, column layout, page density, image scaling, and PDF rendering. Consider a responsive HTML version or a mobile-friendly document layout.

1. Purpose and Scope

The MedOrbits Career Resources library provides practical, accessible educational materials to help students, graduates, interns, job seekers, and professionals prepare for employment and career development.

Resources may include downloadable guides, editable templates, checklists, worksheets, and planning tools covering:

* Resume and CV preparation
* Cover letters and job-application emails
* Interview preparation and follow-up
* Job-search planning and vacancy verification
* Internship applications and preparation
* Workplace communication and professional conduct
* Career planning and professional development
* Job-offer evaluation and joining preparation

This directory stores public documents and their supporting documentation. It does not contain application logic, confidential candidate records, or private recruitment data.

Publication principle: A resource must be created, reviewed, and approved before it is presented as a published MedOrbits resource. A filename or manifest entry alone does not establish publication readiness.

4. Resource Design and Content Standards

Every resource should have a clear purpose, an identifiable audience, and a practical outcome.

Where appropriate, include:

1. Title and purpose: Explain what the resource helps the reader accomplish.
2. Intended audience: Identify the relevant user group or experience level.
3. Actionable guidance: Provide useful steps, examples, exercises, or checklists.
4. Customization instructions: Explain which details users should adapt to their circumstances.
5. Sources: Reference reliable sources for factual, regulated, or time-sensitive guidance.
6. Review information: Record the review date when relevant.
7. Limitations: Explain where requirements differ by employer, industry, location, or individual circumstances.

Content integrity

* Use clearly labelled fictional examples where examples are needed.
* Never fabricate real qualifications, employment history, credentials, vacancies, salary figures, placement statistics, testimonials, or endorsements.
* Do not promise interviews, internships, placements, employment, salary levels, or recruitment outcomes.
* Distinguish general career advice from employer-specific requirements.
* Explain specialized terminology in language appropriate to the intended audience.
* Avoid unnecessary personal data in templates and examples.

Templates should help users present their genuine qualifications and experience accurately.

5. File Formats and Naming

Choose the file format that best supports the resource’s purpose.

Format	Preferred use
PDF	Published guides, checklists, and print-ready resources
DOCX	Editable resumes, cover letters, and worksheets
XLSX	Application trackers and planning spreadsheets
CSV	Portable tabular data, where appropriate
Markdown	Repository documentation and resource instructions

Naming conventions

Use lowercase, descriptive, hyphen-separated filenames.

Examples:

* resume-writing-guide.pdf
* resume-template-editable.docx
* cover-letter-writing-guide.pdf
* interview-preparation-guide.pdf
* job-application-tracker.xlsx
* internship-application-guide.pdf
* professional-communication-guide.pdf

Follow these rules:

* Use one clear topic per filename.
* Avoid spaces, ambiguous abbreviations, and unnecessary special characters.
* Never include private personal information in filenames.
* Avoid names such as final2.pdf or new-document.pdf.
* Use consistent academic-year or version identifiers when multiple editions must be retained.
* Update all references when a resource is renamed, moved, replaced, or withdrawn.

Do not overwrite a published resource without checking whether existing links, historical editions, or dependent pages need updating.

6. Accessibility and Document Quality

All published resources should be readable, accessible, and usable across common devices.

Document requirements

* Use meaningful document titles and a logical heading hierarchy.
* Choose readable fonts and sufficient text and interface contrast.
* Prefer selectable text over image-only PDFs.
* Use descriptive hyperlinks and accessible tables.
* Check page breaks, margins, tables, headers, footers, and exported content.
* Ensure editable templates work in compatible word-processing or spreadsheet applications.
* Test documents on mobile screens and when printed.
* Explain specialized terminology where necessary.
* Check that interactive spreadsheet cells, instructions, and formulas are understandable.
* Avoid conveying essential information through colour alone.

The MedOrbits Royal Burgundy and Champagne Gold design system may be used for branded resources. Readability, accessibility, and print usability take priority over decorative styling.

7. Accuracy and Review

Before publication, review each resource for accuracy, relevance, and potential harm.

* Verify factual claims and external references.
* Review time-sensitive information, application instructions, and eligibility criteria.
* Distinguish general advice from employer-specific or jurisdiction-specific requirements.
* Use authoritative sources for government-related or regulated information where applicable.
* Remove obsolete, misleading, or unsupported claims.
* Confirm that examples are clearly illustrative and factually sound.
* Record review dates for resources likely to become outdated.
* Review external links periodically and remove broken or misleading links.
* Identify historical information clearly when it remains useful for reference.

Do not describe a vacancy, programme, deadline, or opportunity as current unless it has been appropriately verified.

Resources involving employment law, immigration, professional licensing, or other regulated matters must not present general guidance as a substitute for official requirements or qualified professional advice.

8. Privacy, Security, and Copyright

Treat this directory as publicly accessible whenever its contents are served as static website assets.

Never store the following here:

* Private resumes or completed job applications
* Identity documents, passports, certificates, or confidential records
* Personal contact details belonging to real applicants
* Private interview notes or candidate assessments
* Confidential employer documents or unpublished recruitment records
* Passwords, API keys, access tokens, or other secrets

Use fictional placeholders in public templates, for example:

* [Full Name]
* [Email Address]
* [Phone Number]
* [Company Name]
* [Job Title]

Before publishing third-party material, obtain appropriate authorization and comply with applicable copyright and licensing requirements. Review testimonials, photographs, employer logos, and branded materials before use.

Private candidate records, applications, and employer submissions must be stored in appropriately protected systems. Access restrictions must be enforced by the server; hiding a download link does not protect a publicly accessible file.

9. Manifest and Inventory Management

MANIFEST.json is the central inventory for this resource collection.

Maintain it whenever files or categories change.

Manifest requirements

* Declare the resource root consistently.
* List the root README and actual resource files.
* Keep category inventories consistent with the master file list.
* Use unique, normalized relative paths.
* Ensure every listed file exists before release.
* Validate that file extensions match the supported formats.
* Count files accurately; do not count directories as files.
* Keep category counts and total counts synchronized.
* Remove stale entries when resources are deleted.
* Do not claim that a manifest proves content accuracy, file accessibility, or publication approval.

Use paths relative to the declared resource root unless the manifest explicitly specifies otherwise.

For example, the resource entry:

resume-cv/resume-writing-guide.pdf

refers to:

assets/documents/career-resources/resume-cv/resume-writing-guide.pdf

A manifest is descriptive metadata. It is not an access-control mechanism, a content-verification system, or a replacement for server-side validation.

10. Website Integration

Career resources may be linked from relevant MedOrbits pages, including career guidance, jobs, internships, resume assistance, and professional development.

Integration requirements

* Provide descriptive resource titles and concise summaries.
* Display file formats and file sizes where useful.
* Confirm that the target file exists at the expected deployment path.
* Test download links on mobile and desktop.
* Use the correct public URL for the deployed project.
* Apply appropriate access controls to non-public content.
* Update references when files are moved, renamed, replaced, or withdrawn.
* Avoid exposing private documents through public download URLs.
* Provide an understandable fallback when a resource is unavailable.

Example URL, subject to deployment configuration:

/assets/documents/career-resources/resume-cv/resume-writing-guide.pdf

Verify this path against the actual deployed website before using it in HTML, JavaScript, or resource listings.

Only resources approved for publication should appear as active public downloads.

11. Validation and Quality Assurance

Run the repository’s configured checks before submitting resource changes.

npm run check:json
npm run check:data
npm run lint:check
npm run format:check
npm test

These commands require their corresponding scripts and test suites to exist and be configured correctly.

JSON syntax validation does not automatically confirm that:

* All manifest paths point to existing files
* Every PDF opens successfully
* Documents are accessible
* External links are trustworthy
* Content is accurate
* Resources have been approved for publication

Implement or use a dedicated manifest validator to check file existence, unique paths, category membership, extension rules, and count consistency.

Where appropriate, supplement automated validation with manual document review and browser testing.

12. Maintenance Workflow

Use the following workflow for new or updated resources.

1. Define the resource’s purpose, intended audience, and expected outcome.
2. Draft or revise the content.
3. Verify factual claims, examples, and references.
4. Review privacy, permissions, copyright, and licensing.
5. Check accessibility and formatting.
6. Apply the established naming conventions.
7. Place the file in the appropriate category.
8. Update the category README and MANIFEST.json.
9. Run the applicable validation checks.
10. Test the deployed URL and download behaviour.
11. Approve the resource before making it publicly available.
12. Review, replace, withdraw, or archive outdated editions as appropriate.

Keep related documentation and website-link changes in the same change set where practical.

15. Scope and Disclaimer

This README documents the purpose, structure, and recommended maintenance practices for assets/documents/career-resources/.

It does not independently verify the existence, accuracy, accessibility, or publication status of individual files. Those properties must be established through the repository’s validation and review processes.

Career resources provide general educational and professional guidance. They do not guarantee interviews, internships, employment, salaries, placements, or recruitment outcomes.

Users should verify role-specific requirements with employers and consult appropriate official authorities or qualified professionals when necessary.

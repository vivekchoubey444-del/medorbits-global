1. Overview

The job-search/ directory contains practical resources designed to help students, fresh graduates, and experienced professionals organize their job search, identify suitable opportunities, manage applications, evaluate employment offers, and recognize potential recruitment scams.

These resources support MedOrbits’ career guidance, job assistance, internship support, employment preparation, and recruitment services.

The directory is intended for reusable educational materials and blank templates. Personal application records, confidential correspondence, and completed trackers must remain outside the public assets directory.

Objectives

* Help candidates establish realistic career and job-search goals.
* Improve the organization and consistency of job applications.
* Encourage independent verification of employers, vacancies, and recruiters.
* Support informed evaluation of employment offers.
* Promote responsible handling of personal and employment-related information.
* Encourage systematic follow-up without guaranteeing recruitment outcomes.

5. Job Search Workflow

Resources should encourage candidates to follow a structured and repeatable process.

Step 1 — Define Career Goals

* Identify preferred job roles, industries, and career pathways.
* Review qualifications, skills, experience, and professional interests.
* Establish realistic location, salary, and work-arrangement preferences.
* Identify skills or qualifications that may need further development.
* Set achievable weekly job-search targets.

Step 2 — Find Suitable Opportunities

* Use reputable job portals and official employer career pages.
* Review job descriptions, eligibility requirements, and experience criteria.
* Check application deadlines and required documents.
* Confirm that the role aligns with the candidate’s qualifications and objectives.
* Independently verify vacancies before sharing personal information or submitting documents.

Step 3 — Prepare Applications

* Customize resumes and cover letters for relevant roles.
* Provide accurate information about qualifications, employment history, and achievements.
* Follow the employer’s application instructions.
* Submit applications through official or otherwise verified channels.
* Retain copies of submitted documents where appropriate and secure.

Step 4 — Track Progress

* Record the employer, job title, application date, and vacancy URL.
* Track application status and interview dates.
* Record follow-up dates and the next required action.
* Update records when an employer responds or the application status changes.
* Remove outdated entries when they are no longer required.

Step 5 — Evaluate Offers

* Review job responsibilities, reporting arrangements, working hours, and location.
* Compare compensation, benefits, leave provisions, and other relevant terms.
* Distinguish gross salary, take-home pay, fixed compensation, and variable compensation.
* Review written employment terms, probation requirements, and notice periods.
* Clarify ambiguous conditions with the employer before accepting.
* Seek qualified advice for significant contractual, financial, or legal questions.

Candidates should assess the complete employment arrangement rather than making decisions based on salary or job title alone.

6. Job Application Tracker Standards

The job-application-tracker.xlsx workbook should be a reusable template suitable for individual job seekers.

Recommended fields

* Employer name.
* Job title.
* Vacancy URL.
* Application date.
* Application status.
* Interview date, if applicable.
* Follow-up date.
* Next action.
* Notes limited to necessary application information.

Recommended usability features

Where appropriate, the workbook may include:

* Dropdown lists for application status.
* Consistent date formatting.
* Filters for employer, role, status, or deadline.
* Clearly labeled columns and instructions.
* A sample row containing fictional information.
* A print-friendly layout.
* An editable structure compatible with commonly used spreadsheet applications.

Privacy requirements

* Never include passwords, one-time passcodes, bank details, identity numbers, or unnecessary sensitive personal information.
* Do not use real candidate records as sample data.
* Keep completed trackers in the user’s private storage.
* Never publish completed application trackers containing personal or confidential information in the public assets directory.
* Do not enable external data connections or macros unless they are necessary, reviewed, and explicitly documented.

The published workbook should be a blank template or contain clearly identified fictional sample data only.

7. Employer and Vacancy Verification

Candidates should independently verify opportunities before applying, paying fees, sharing sensitive information, or accepting an offer.

Verification checklist

Candidates should review:

* Employer identity: Confirm the organization’s name, official website, and publicly available contact details.
* Vacancy authenticity: Look for the role on the employer’s official careers page or verify it through an authorized recruiter.
* Recruiter identity: Check the recruiter’s professional identity, email domain, and contact information using independently obtained details.
* Job description: Review responsibilities, eligibility criteria, location, compensation information, and application procedures.
* Application channel: Confirm that the application destination is appropriate for the employer or authorized recruitment organization.
* Offer documentation: Verify the issuing organization, written terms, compensation, conditions, and authorized contact before accepting.

Recruitment warning signs

Exercise caution when a recruiter or organization:

* Requests payment to secure a job, interview, offer letter, or guaranteed placement.
* Promises guaranteed employment without a credible selection process.
* Pressures candidates to make immediate payments or decisions.
* Uses inconsistent organization names, suspicious contact details, or unverifiable vacancy information.
* Requests unnecessary sensitive information at an early stage.
* Sends unexpected attachments or links that cannot be independently verified.

Requests for payment connected to recruitment are significant warning signs. Candidates should verify any claimed fee independently and should not assume that a payment request is legitimate merely because it appears on a professional-looking website.

Important: HTTPS encrypts a connection but does not, by itself, establish that a website, employer, recruiter, or vacancy is legitimate.

8. Content Quality Standards

All published resources must:

* Use accurate, clear, inclusive, and professional language.
* Distinguish general advice from employer-specific requirements.
* Avoid fabricated vacancies, employers, salary figures, testimonials, or recruitment claims.
* Avoid guaranteeing interviews, employment offers, placements, or hiring outcomes.
* Identify external resources and references accurately.
* Include appropriate vacancy-verification and privacy guidance.
* Avoid presenting outdated procedures as current.
* Be reviewed when relevant laws, recruitment practices, external links, or platform procedures change.

Do not describe a vacancy as current unless its availability has been verified through an appropriate source. If a resource contains examples, label fictional or illustrative examples clearly.

11. Privacy and Security

This directory is intended for public educational resources.

Publication safeguards

* Keep personal application records outside the public assets directory.
* Never publish completed trackers, private recruiter correspondence, or confidential employment offers.
* Remove unnecessary document metadata before publication.
* Never include passwords, API keys, access tokens, or other secrets.
* Use fictional sample data in publicly distributed templates.
* Store private recruitment and candidate records in appropriately access-controlled systems.
* Share personal information only when necessary and through suitable, verified channels.
* Review spreadsheet formulas, links, macros, and embedded objects before publication.

Public resources must not expose candidate records or imply that private application information is collected by MedOrbits unless an explicitly documented and authorized service requires it.

15. Scope and Disclaimer

These resources provide general educational and career guidance. They do not guarantee the availability or legitimacy of any particular vacancy, interviews, employment offers, recruitment outcomes, or placement.

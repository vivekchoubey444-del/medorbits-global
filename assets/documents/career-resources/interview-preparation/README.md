1. Overview

The interview-preparation/ directory contains structured educational materials that help students, fresh graduates, experienced professionals, and other job seekers prepare for employment interviews, internship interviews, technical assessments, and professional selection processes.

These resources support MedOrbits’ career guidance, job assistance, internship support, and recruitment services.

The materials should help candidates research opportunities, communicate their genuine qualifications, practise relevant questions, evaluate feedback, and approach interviews with confidence and professionalism.

Publication principle: Only resources that have been created, reviewed, and approved should be presented as published MedOrbits downloads. A file’s presence in the directory or manifest does not establish that its contents have been verified.

5. Interview Preparation Framework

5.1 Before the interview

Resources should help candidates:

* Research the employer, role, and job description using reliable sources.
* Review relevant qualifications, projects, experience, and skills.
* Prepare truthful examples demonstrating their abilities and achievements.
* Practise common questions and role-specific scenarios.
* Identify areas where further technical or professional preparation is needed.
* Confirm the interview date, time zone, location, format, and required documents.
* Test internet connectivity, audio, camera, and equipment for virtual interviews.
* Prepare relevant questions about the position and its expectations.
* Plan travel and arrival time for in-person interviews.

Candidates should verify interview arrangements through appropriate employer communication channels when details are unclear.

5.2 During the interview

Resources should encourage candidates to:

* Communicate clearly, respectfully, and professionally.
* Listen carefully and ask for clarification when necessary.
* Support answers with relevant, truthful examples.
* Describe individual contributions accurately, including work completed as part of a team.
* Acknowledge knowledge gaps honestly instead of inventing answers.
* Respect confidentiality and follow the interviewer’s instructions.
* Ask relevant questions about responsibilities, expectations, supervision, and working arrangements.
* Follow applicable assessment rules and avoid misrepresenting work completed independently.

5.3 After the interview

Resources should explain how to:

* Send a concise thank-you message when appropriate.
* Follow the employer’s stated communication timeline.
* Send a polite follow-up when the expected response date has passed.
* Record feedback and identify areas for improvement.
* Track application status and next steps.
* Keep interview notes, correspondence, and personal feedback private.

Candidates should avoid repeated or excessive follow-ups and respect the employer’s stated communication preferences.

6. Question and Answer Standards

Practice materials should provide useful preparation without implying that a particular employer will ask specific questions.

Include relevant categories where appropriate:

* General and introductory questions
* Behavioural questions
* Situational and problem-solving questions
* Technical or subject-specific questions
* Role-specific competency questions
* Internship and entry-level questions
* Questions candidates may ask the interviewer

Structured response techniques

Explain the STAR method where useful:

* Situation: Describe the relevant context.
* Task: Explain the responsibility or objective.
* Action: Describe the steps you personally took.
* Result: Explain the outcome, learning, or measurable impact where supported by evidence.

Candidates should use real experiences from employment, education, projects, volunteering, or other relevant activities. They should not invent results or claim another person’s work as their own.

Encourage candidates to adapt their answers naturally rather than memorizing scripts that misrepresent their experience.

Interview formats, evaluation criteria, and question selection vary by employer and role.

7. Mock Interview Worksheet Standards

The worksheet should provide blank or generic fields for:

* Target role and interview date
* Interview format
* Practice questions
* Candidate responses and supporting examples
* Communication and technical knowledge assessments
* Identified strengths and improvement areas
* Feedback and next steps
* Follow-up preparation goals

The worksheet should be usable for individual practice, mentoring sessions, or authorized mock interviews.

Do not publish completed worksheets containing identifiable candidate information, actual recruitment assessments, confidential interview notes, or restricted employer materials.

8. File Naming and Version Control

Use lowercase, descriptive, hyphen-separated filenames.

Preferred examples:

* interview-preparation-guide.pdf
* common-interview-questions.pdf
* mock-interview-worksheet.pdf
* interview-follow-up-guide.pdf

Avoid spaces, ambiguous abbreviations, unnecessary version numbers, and filenames containing personal information.

When a resource requires a significant revision:

1. Review the existing content and dependencies.
2. Update the material and verify relevant references.
3. Check document formatting and accessibility.
4. Update the parent MANIFEST.json and affected website links.
5. Preserve previous editions when there is a legitimate historical or operational reason.
6. Confirm that the deployed file matches the approved version.

Record review dates and version information in the document or manifest where appropriate.

9. Accessibility and Compatibility

All published resources should be readable, accessible, and usable across common devices.

* Use readable typography and a logical heading hierarchy.
* Maintain sufficient text and visual contrast.
* Provide selectable text in PDFs wherever possible.
* Use descriptive hyperlinks and accessible tables.
* Ensure documents display correctly on mobile devices and when printed.
* Test worksheet fields, writing space, page breaks, and margins.
* Use meaningful document titles and appropriate metadata.
* Explain specialized interview terminology in plain language.
* Avoid relying on colour alone to communicate important information.

Apply the MedOrbits Royal Burgundy and Champagne Gold design direction where appropriate without compromising readability or accessibility.

10. Privacy, Security, and Copyright

This directory is intended exclusively for approved public educational resources.

Never publish:

* Completed mock interview worksheets containing personal information.
* Private candidate assessments, interview notes, or recruitment records.
* Identity documents, confidential correspondence, or private contact details.
* Confidential interview questions obtained through restricted channels.
* Employer or candidate information that has not been authorized for publication.
* Passwords, API keys, access tokens, or other secrets.

Use fictional examples and clearly marked placeholders in public templates.

Remove unnecessary personal metadata before publication. Obtain appropriate authorization before using third-party text, illustrations, logos, or other protected materials.

Private interview notes and candidate assessments must be stored in an appropriately protected system. Hiding a download link does not secure a publicly accessible file.

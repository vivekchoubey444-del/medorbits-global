1. Overview

The internships/ directory contains educational guides, checklists, and career-development resources that help students, graduates, and early-career professionals explore internship opportunities and prepare for the application process.

These resources support MedOrbits’ career guidance, internship assistance, job-readiness, and recruitment services.

The materials should help candidates identify relevant opportunities, prepare accurate applications, assess internship arrangements, recognize potential recruitment scams, and approach professional experiences responsibly.

Publication principle: Only resources that have been created, reviewed, and approved should be presented as published MedOrbits downloads. A file’s presence in the directory or manifest does not establish that its contents have been verified.

5. Internship Application Guidance

Resources should provide practical guidance on the following stages.

Opportunity discovery

* Identify internships relevant to academic qualifications and career goals.
* Use employer career pages, recognized platforms, educational institutions, and other appropriate sources.
* Review the role description, eligibility requirements, responsibilities, duration, location, and application deadline.
* Distinguish verified openings from expired, unconfirmed, or potentially fraudulent listings.

Application preparation

* Prepare a role-specific resume and cover letter where required.
* Highlight genuine skills, projects, coursework, certifications, and relevant experience.
* Follow the employer’s requested application format and submission instructions.
* Check document names, file formats, contact details, and attachments before submission.
* Avoid including sensitive personal information unless it is legitimately required through an appropriate process.

Interviews and assessments

* Research the employer using reliable sources.
* Review the internship description and relevant technical or professional topics.
* Practise explaining genuine projects, academic work, skills, and learning goals.
* Prepare thoughtful questions about responsibilities, supervision, training, and expected outcomes.
* Communicate professionally during interviews and assessments.
* Send an appropriate follow-up message when suitable.

Application tracking

Encourage candidates to maintain a record of applications, dates, interview stages, follow-ups, and outcomes.

Personal tracking tools should be kept private when they contain contact details, correspondence, or other personal information.

6. Internship Evaluation and Candidate Safety

Before accepting an internship, candidates should assess the opportunity and clarify relevant terms.

Area	What to verify
Employer identity	Organization name, official website, and credible contact details
Role responsibilities	Duties, deliverables, expected learning outcomes, and evaluation criteria
Supervision	Supervisor, mentor, reporting structure, and feedback process
Duration and schedule	Start date, end date, working hours, location, and attendance expectations
Compensation	Stipend, salary if applicable, expenses, payment schedule, or unpaid status
Written terms	Offer letter, agreement, responsibilities, and applicable conditions
Academic requirements	Institutional approval, academic credit, or placement documentation where applicable
Data and documents	Why requested information is needed, how it will be submitted, and how it will be handled
Costs and commitments	Any fees, deposits, equipment purchases, training charges, or other obligations

Warning signs

Encourage candidates to exercise caution when an opportunity involves:

* Requests for recruitment or placement fees without a clearly verified and legitimate basis.
* Pressure to accept an offer immediately.
* Unverifiable employer identities or suspicious communication channels.
* Requests for passwords, authentication codes, or unnecessary identity or financial documents.
* Unclear responsibilities, compensation, working hours, or reporting arrangements.
* Promises of guaranteed employment or unrealistic compensation without credible supporting information.

Candidates should independently verify the organization and clarify unclear terms before proceeding.

Do not make unsupported legal claims. Employment protections, compensation requirements, internship classifications, and other obligations may vary by jurisdiction and arrangement. Refer candidates to relevant official authorities or qualified professionals when necessary.

7. Content Quality Standards

All published resources must:

* Use accurate, clear, and professional language.
* Provide practical, actionable guidance.
* Avoid fabricated internship openings, employers, qualifications, or experience.
* Avoid guaranteeing internship placement, compensation, academic credit, or future employment.
* Distinguish general guidance from employer-specific requirements.
* Clearly identify illustrative examples.
* Avoid exposing real candidates’ personal information.
* Reference reliable sources for factual or regulated guidance.
* Be reviewed when relevant application procedures, regulations, or external links change.
* Identify the intended audience and explain the resource’s limitations where appropriate.

Resources should help candidates make informed decisions rather than encourage them to accept opportunities without adequate review.

8. File Naming and Version Control

Use lowercase, descriptive, hyphen-separated filenames.

Preferred examples:

* internship-application-guide.pdf
* internship-interview-guide.pdf
* internship-preparation-checklist.pdf
* internship-resume-checklist.pdf

Avoid spaces, ambiguous abbreviations, unnecessary version numbers, and filenames containing personal information.

When a resource needs substantial revision:

1. Review the existing content and its dependencies.
2. Update the content and verify relevant references.
3. Check document quality and accessibility.
4. Update the parent manifest and affected website links.
5. Preserve previous editions when there is a legitimate historical or operational reason.
6. Confirm that the deployed resource matches the approved version.

Record version and review information in the document or manifest where appropriate.

9. Accessibility and Compatibility

All published resources should be readable, accessible, and usable across common devices.

* Use readable typography and a logical heading hierarchy.
* Maintain sufficient contrast.
* Provide selectable text in PDFs wherever possible.
* Use descriptive hyperlinks and accessible tables.
* Ensure documents display correctly on mobile devices and when printed.
* Avoid unnecessary decorative elements that make checklists difficult to print or complete.
* Check page breaks, margins, text overflow, and list formatting.
* Use meaningful document titles and appropriate document metadata.
* Ensure the content remains understandable without relying on colour alone.

Apply the MedOrbits Royal Burgundy and Champagne Gold design direction where appropriate without compromising readability or accessibility.

10. Privacy, Security, and Copyright

This directory is intended exclusively for approved public educational materials.

Never store or publish:

* Candidate resumes containing private personal information.
* Identity documents, passports, academic records, or confidential certificates.
* Internship offers or private correspondence belonging to real candidates.
* Confidential employer documents or recruitment records.
* Passwords, API keys, access tokens, or other secrets.

Remove unnecessary personal metadata before public release. Use clearly marked fictional examples and placeholders in templates.

Obtain appropriate authorization before publishing third-party text, images, logos, or other protected material. Respect applicable copyright and licensing requirements.

Private applications, candidate records, and recruitment submissions must be stored in appropriately protected systems. Access controls must be enforced by the server; hiding a download link does not protect a publicly accessible file.

These resources provide general educational and career guidance. They do not guarantee internship opportunities, selection, compensation, academic credit, or employment.

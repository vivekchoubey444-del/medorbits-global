1. Overview

The cover-letters/ directory contains professional cover-letter guidance, editable templates, and job-application email resources. These materials help students, graduates, experienced professionals, internship applicants, and other job seekers prepare accurate, relevant, and professional employment applications.

The resources support MedOrbits’ career guidance, job assistance, internship support, and recruitment services.

All materials should emphasize truthful representation, role-specific customization, professional communication, and respect for employer application instructions.

A resource must be reviewed and approved before being presented as a published MedOrbits download. Its presence in the directory or manifest does not, by itself, establish publication approval.

4. Intended Audience

These resources may support:

* Students applying for their first job.
* Fresh graduates entering the workforce.
* Experienced professionals seeking new opportunities.
* Internship and trainee applicants.
* Career changers adapting their application materials.
* Candidates applying for healthcare, education, IT, engineering, management, and other professional roles.

Examples and instructions should account for differences in experience level, industry, employer expectations, and application method.

5. Content Standards

All resources must follow these principles:

* Use clear, concise, professional language.
* Tailor application materials to the target position and employer.
* Highlight relevant skills, qualifications, projects, and achievements supported by the applicant’s actual experience.
* Explain how to adapt content for different industries and experience levels.
* Mark editable placeholders clearly.
* Avoid fabricated credentials, employment history, achievements, references, or employer information.
* Avoid unnecessary repetition of the resume or CV.
* Encourage proofreading, consistency, and compliance with application instructions.
* Avoid guaranteeing interviews, employment, or selection.

Use fictional examples when demonstrating application language. Clearly identify them as examples rather than real applicant histories or verified employment outcomes.

6. Recommended Cover Letter Structure

Where the employer requests a cover letter, resources should explain the following components.

1. Applicant details: Contact information, where appropriate and requested.
2. Date and recipient: Include when relevant to the required format.
3. Greeting: Use an appropriate professional greeting.
4. Opening paragraph: Identify the position and explain the applicant’s interest.
5. Relevant evidence: Present suitable skills, qualifications, experience, projects, or achievements.
6. Role alignment: Explain how the applicant’s genuine experience relates to the position.
7. Closing paragraph: Express interest in further discussion without making unsupported claims.
8. Sign-off: Use an appropriate professional closing.

Applicants should follow the employer’s instructions whenever a different format, application form, or email-only submission is required.

7. Job-Application Email Standards

The email guide should explain how to prepare a concise, professional application message.

Where applicable, include:

* A clear subject line.
* An appropriate greeting.
* The position title and relevant reference number.
* A brief introduction and statement of interest.
* A concise explanation of relevant qualifications.
* A clear list of attached documents.
* A professional closing and appropriate contact details.

Applicants should confirm that attachments are correct, readable, appropriately named, and requested by the employer.

Do not advise applicants to send sensitive identity documents or financial records unless the recipient and submission process have been appropriately verified and the information is genuinely required.

8. File Naming and Version Control

Use lowercase, descriptive, hyphen-separated filenames.

Preferred examples:

* cover-letter-writing-guide.pdf
* cover-letter-template.docx
* job-application-email-guide.pdf

Avoid spaces, ambiguous abbreviations, unnecessary version numbers, and filenames containing personal information.

When significant revisions are needed:

1. Review the existing resource and its dependencies.
2. Update the content and perform the required checks.
3. Preserve previous editions when there is a legitimate historical or operational reason.
4. Update the parent manifest and affected website links.
5. Verify that the deployed file is the intended version.

Use document metadata or manifest fields to record version and review information where appropriate.

9. Accessibility and Compatibility

All published resources should be usable across common devices and compatible applications.

* Use readable fonts and consistent formatting.
* Maintain sufficient text and visual contrast.
* Use meaningful headings and descriptive link text.
* Prefer selectable text in PDFs.
* Verify that PDFs display correctly on mobile devices and when printed.
* Ensure DOCX templates remain editable in compatible word-processing applications.
* Avoid fixed layouts that break when users replace placeholder text.
* Use accessible document structure where supported.
* Check page breaks, margins, spacing, and text overflow.
* Avoid communicating important information through colour alone.

Apply the MedOrbits Royal Burgundy and Champagne Gold design direction where appropriate, without compromising readability or accessibility.

10. Privacy, Security, and Copyright

This directory is intended for public resources and must be treated as publicly accessible when served as static website content.

Never publish:

* Completed applications containing real applicants’ private information.
* Private contact details, signatures, identity documents, or confidential correspondence.
* Personal employment records or private reference details.
* Passwords, API keys, access tokens, or other secrets.

Use clearly marked fictional placeholders, such as:

* [Full Name]
* [Email Address]
* [Phone Number]
* [Company Name]
* [Job Title]

Remove unnecessary personal information and review document metadata before public release.

Obtain appropriate authorization for third-party text, illustrations, photographs, logos, and other protected material. Respect applicable copyright and licensing requirements.

Private resumes and application records must be stored in an appropriately protected system. Hiding a link does not secure a publicly accessible document.

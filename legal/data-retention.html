Data Retention.

Learn how MedOrbits approaches the retention, review, archiving and deletion of personal information and service records. This policy explains why some information may need to be retained, what factors influence retention periods and how to raise a request concerning information associated with your service.

Retention periods depend on the record and applicable law. This policy does not invent fixed deletion deadlines or claim that all records are automatically deleted after a service ends. Actual retention schedules should be based on the business's real systems, operational needs and applicable legal obligations.

1. Purpose and scope

This Data Retention Policy describes the principles for deciding how long information associated with MedOrbits should be kept and when it should be reviewed, deleted, anonymised or otherwise securely disposed of.

It may apply to information processed in connection with website enquiries, education guidance, admission support, career services, application preparation, uploaded documents, transactions, customer communications and technical operations. The information actually retained depends on the service used and the systems involved.

This policy should be read alongside the Privacy Policy and Data Protection Policy. A service-specific notice or applicable law may establish additional requirements.

2. Core retention principles

Information should not be kept indefinitely merely because it was once collected. Retention decisions should be proportionate to the purpose, type of record and relevant legal or operational requirements.

  • Purpose limitation: retain information for an identified and legitimate reason.
  • Data minimisation: avoid keeping unnecessary copies or unrelated information.
  • Defined review: review records according to an appropriate schedule where practicable.
  • Legal compliance: account for applicable record-keeping, regulatory and legal requirements.
  • Security: protect retained records against unauthorised access, loss or disclosure.
  • Secure disposal: delete or otherwise dispose of information when retention is no longer justified.
  • Accountability: document relevant retention rules and exceptions where appropriate.

3. Categories of records

Depending on the services provided and the features in use, records may include the following categories.

Enquiries and communications

Contact form submissions, email or other support communications, complaint records and relevant follow-up correspondence.

Education and career records

Course preferences, guidance notes, education-related information, resume details and service records created to support a requested education or career service.

Applications and submitted documents

Application-preparation records, documents voluntarily provided for a service and related processing information. The sensitivity of these records should be considered when setting access and retention rules.

Payment and accounting records

Transaction references, invoices, receipts, refund records and other accounting or service records required for administration, reconciliation or legal compliance.

Security and technical records

Where generated by deployed systems, diagnostic logs, security events, access records and technical information used to maintain website functionality or investigate suspected misuse.

4. What determines a retention period?

A retention period should be determined by the purpose of the record and the requirements applicable to that category. Relevant factors may include:

  • How long the requested service remains active.
  • Whether the record is needed to respond to a current enquiry or complete a service.
  • Applicable statutory or regulatory record-keeping requirements.
  • Accounting, tax, payment reconciliation and audit needs.
  • Whether a complaint, dispute, investigation or legal claim remains unresolved.
  • Security requirements and the need to investigate suspected fraud or unauthorised access.
  • Whether information can be anonymised or safely deleted without affecting a lawful requirement.
  • Whether a user has made a valid request concerning the information.

The business should maintain a retention schedule appropriate to its actual records and systems. This page does not substitute for that operational schedule.

5. Retention schedule and transparency

Where appropriate, MedOrbits should document retention periods or criteria for each major record category. Specific periods should be published only after they have been checked against applicable law, business operations and actual system capabilities.

Different record types may have different retention requirements. For example, a temporary enquiry may not need to be kept for the same period as a transaction record that must be retained for accounting or legal purposes.

Where a precise period cannot reasonably be determined in advance, the applicable privacy notice should explain the criteria used to decide when the information is no longer required, where required by law.

Implementation requirement

Before publishing exact timeframes, create and verify a record-retention schedule covering databases, uploaded files, email, backups, logs and third-party services used by the business.

6. Enquiries and service records

Enquiry and service records may be retained for the time needed to respond to a request, provide follow-up support, maintain an appropriate service history or address a related complaint.

When the purpose has ended, records should be reviewed for deletion, anonymisation or another appropriate disposition unless a valid reason for further retention remains.

A completed enquiry does not necessarily require every associated record to be deleted immediately. A record may still be needed for a pending dispute, a legal obligation or another legitimate and lawful purpose.

7. Uploaded documents and application records

Educational certificates, identity documents, resumes and other uploaded files may contain personal or high-risk information. Such records should be retained only for a justified purpose and protected through appropriate access controls.

Good retention practices may include:

  • Defining which documents are necessary for each service.
  • Limiting access to authorised personnel or systems.
  • Avoiding unnecessary duplicate copies.
  • Separating active service files from records no longer needed for routine processing.
  • Reviewing documents for deletion when their purpose has ended.
  • Preserving records only where a legal, security or dispute-related reason justifies it.

Do not assume that uploading a document makes it part of a permanent record. Equally, do not assume that a file is deleted automatically when a form is submitted or a service is completed. The actual process depends on the deployed system and applicable retention rules.

8. Payment, accounting and transaction records

Transaction records may need to be retained for payment reconciliation, invoices, refunds, accounting, tax compliance, fraud prevention, dispute resolution or other lawful business purposes.

The retention period may be influenced by the relevant legal requirements and the nature of the transaction. Records should not be kept longer than necessary once all applicable obligations and legitimate purposes have been satisfied.

Payment credentials should not be retained unless there is a lawful, necessary and appropriately secured basis for doing so. Sensitive authentication information, such as OTPs and payment PINs, should not be requested for ordinary support or stored as routine enquiry data.

For transaction-related questions, consult the Payment Policy and Refund Policy.

9. Support communications and complaints

Communications may be retained to respond to a request, document a service decision, manage a complaint or establish what was communicated about a transaction.

A complaint or dispute may justify retaining relevant correspondence until the matter is resolved and any applicable legal retention period has expired. Access should remain limited to people who need the records for an authorised purpose.

Unrelated personal information contained in a message should not be copied into other records unnecessarily. Where feasible and appropriate, records should be organised so that information can be located and disposed of in accordance with the applicable schedule.

10. Technical, access and security logs

Depending on the deployed infrastructure, technical logs may be generated to diagnose faults, maintain system reliability, detect suspicious activity or investigate security incidents.

Logs can contain identifiers, timestamps, IP addresses or other technical information that may qualify as personal information in certain circumstances. Access and retention should therefore be proportionate to the operational or security purpose.

Logs should not be retained indefinitely without a justified reason. A security incident or investigation may require particular records to be preserved longer than the ordinary operational period, subject to applicable law.

11. Backups, archives and redundant copies

Backups and archives can preserve information beyond its active use. A deletion process should consider whether information also exists in backup systems, replicated storage, exports or archived records.

The time and method required to remove information from backups may differ from the process used for active databases. Where immediate removal from a backup is not technically feasible, an appropriate process should restrict ordinary use and ensure the information is handled in accordance with the applicable retention and deletion requirements.

Backup cycles and deletion capabilities should be documented based on the actual infrastructure. This policy does not claim that every backup or archived copy is immediately erased when a primary record is deleted.

12. Third-party systems and service providers

Personal information may be processed or retained by external providers supporting website hosting, communications, document management, payments or other service functions, depending on the features used.

Where appropriate and required, agreements and provider settings should address data retention, deletion assistance, security and the handling of information when a service ends.

A deletion request submitted to MedOrbits may require coordination with a provider if the relevant information is stored in that provider's system. The outcome depends on the provider's role, available controls and applicable legal obligations.

Review the Third-Party Links Policy before providing personal information to an external service.

13. Legal holds and unresolved matters

Information that would otherwise be due for deletion may need to be preserved when required by law or when a valid legal claim, dispute, investigation or regulatory matter requires the relevant records.

Such retention should be limited to the records and duration reasonably necessary for the applicable requirement. Where the reason for the hold ends, the records should be reviewed again under the ordinary retention rules.

A request for deletion does not necessarily override mandatory record-keeping duties or a valid requirement to preserve evidence. Any decision should be assessed under applicable law.

14. Deletion and secure disposal

When information is no longer required for a legitimate purpose and no legal or other valid retention requirement applies, it should be deleted, anonymised or otherwise securely disposed of using a method suitable for the record and storage medium.

Depending on the system, appropriate methods may include:

  • Removing records from active databases through authorised procedures.
  • Deleting unnecessary files and duplicate exports.
  • Disposing of physical documents through a secure process.
  • Applying an appropriate deletion or expiry process to archived records.
  • Managing backup copies according to the documented backup lifecycle.
  • Confirming deletion with a service provider where this is necessary and available.

The chosen method should account for the system's technical limitations and the sensitivity of the information. Simply hiding a record from the user interface may not mean that the underlying data has been deleted.

15. Anonymisation and aggregated information

Where suitable, information may be transformed so that it can no longer reasonably be linked to an identifiable individual. Properly anonymised information may be useful for aggregate reporting, service analysis or understanding general usage patterns.

Removing a person's name alone does not necessarily anonymise a record. Other identifiers and combinations of details may still allow identification.

Information that is merely pseudonymised or can still be linked to an individual should continue to receive appropriate protection under applicable law.

16. Requests to access, correct or delete information

You may submit a request concerning your personal information through the official MedOrbits contact page. Identify the relevant service or record where possible and explain the action you are requesting.

Depending on applicable law, available rights may include access, correction, deletion, withdrawal of consent or other forms of data-related request. A request may be subject to identity verification, legal exceptions and retention obligations.

If information must be retained for a valid reason, the business should assess whether the request can be partially fulfilled, whether unnecessary copies can be removed or whether other appropriate measures are available under applicable law.

Do not send passwords, OTPs, banking PINs or payment-card security codes when making a data request.

17. Access to retained records

Retained information should not automatically remain accessible to everyone who could access it during active service delivery. Access should be restricted according to the record's purpose, sensitivity and the user's authorised role.

Appropriate practices may include removing access when a role changes, limiting downloads, protecting stored documents and reviewing permissions where needed. Actual controls should be documented and implemented in the systems used.

Records retained for accounting, legal or security purposes should be used only for those authorised purposes and handled in accordance with applicable privacy requirements.

18. Records relating to children

Education services may involve information about students who are children under applicable law. Such records require particular care, and collection, use, retention and deletion must follow the legal requirements that apply to the service.

Where required, parental or guardian involvement, consent or additional safeguards should be considered. Records relating to a child should not be retained longer than justified by the relevant service, legal obligations or another lawful purpose.

19. Security incidents and evidence preservation

If a security incident, suspected fraud or unauthorised access is identified, relevant records may need to be preserved to investigate the event, reduce harm, meet legal obligations or respond to a lawful request.

Any extended retention should be proportionate to the incident and reviewed when the need for preservation ends. Records should remain protected while an investigation is ongoing.

If you suspect that your personal information has been exposed or misused, contact MedOrbits through its official contact page. Provide relevant details without including passwords or one-time passcodes.

See the Anti-Fraud Policy for additional security guidance.

20. Storage locations and international providers

Depending on the infrastructure and external providers used, information may be stored, backed up or processed in different locations. The actual arrangements should be established from the deployed systems and provider agreements.

Where location-specific or cross-border retention requirements apply, the business should account for them when setting retention and deletion procedures.

This policy does not guarantee that every record is stored in one country or that all third-party systems follow identical deletion schedules.

21. Responsibilities

The business operating MedOrbits should establish appropriate responsibilities for managing records, reviewing retention schedules, handling deletion requests and disposing of information securely.

Personnel and authorised service providers should follow applicable access restrictions and handling procedures. Users should provide accurate information, submit only necessary documents and avoid attempting to access or retain information belonging to others without authority.

Responsibility assignments and operating procedures should reflect the actual size, structure and technical arrangements of the business.

22. Exceptions and compliance

A retention or deletion decision may be affected by applicable law, an enforceable legal obligation, a legitimate dispute, a security investigation or another recognised legal basis.

Exceptions should not be used to justify indefinite retention without a valid reason. Where practical, the scope of an exception should be limited to relevant records and reviewed when circumstances change.

Nothing in this policy is intended to reduce rights that cannot lawfully be excluded or to replace mandatory requirements under applicable law.

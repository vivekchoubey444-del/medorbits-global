Refund Policy.

This policy explains how refund requests relating to MedOrbits services and payments should be submitted, reviewed and resolved. It outlines the factors that may affect eligibility, how duplicate or incorrect payments are handled, and what to expect when a refund is approved. The terms agreed for a specific service, applicable law and verified transaction details remain important.

1. Purpose and scope

This Refund Policy describes the framework for handling requests to return payments made for services offered by MedOrbits. It applies to relevant consultancy, education guidance, career support or other paid services where a payment and refund arrangement exists.

The applicable terms may vary according to the service, the work agreed, the stage of delivery, the payment method and any written quotation, invoice, proposal or service agreement provided to the customer.

This policy should be read with the Terms and Conditions, Payment Policy and Cancellation Policy.

2. Refund principles

Refund requests should be assessed consistently and on the basis of the transaction, service terms and relevant facts. The following principles guide the review process:

  • Transparency: applicable fees and any disclosed refund conditions should be communicated before payment where required.
  • Verification: the transaction and the person making the request may need to be verified.
  • Service-based assessment: the work already delivered and any agreed milestones may be relevant.
  • Fair treatment: requests should be reviewed on their merits, without arbitrary or misleading promises.
  • Legal compliance: mandatory consumer protections and other applicable laws take precedence over conflicting policy wording.
  • Record keeping: relevant decisions and transaction references may be recorded for accounting, support or compliance purposes.

3. When may a refund be considered?

A refund may be considered where supported by the applicable service terms, the transaction circumstances or applicable law. Depending on the facts, examples may include:

  • A verified duplicate payment for the same charge.
  • A payment collected incorrectly or for an amount that was not due.
  • A service that was cancelled before the agreed work began, where the applicable terms or law support a refund.
  • A material failure to provide an agreed service, subject to review and any applicable legal remedies.
  • An approved refund under a written quotation, service agreement or other disclosed refund commitment.
  • Another circumstance in which a refund or reimbursement is required by applicable law.

These examples are not an automatic entitlement in every case. The relevant agreement, payment evidence, delivery status and applicable legal requirements must be considered.

4. Fees that may not qualify for a refund

Some charges may relate to work already completed, resources already supplied, third-party costs or other services that have been performed. A refund may be limited or unavailable for such amounts where this treatment was properly disclosed, is supported by the applicable agreement and is lawful.

Depending on the specific service, relevant considerations may include:

  • Consultation sessions or guidance already delivered.
  • Application preparation, document review or other completed work.
  • Clearly disclosed third-party charges that have actually been incurred and cannot reasonably be recovered.
  • Work completed under agreed milestones before a cancellation request.
  • Digital materials or other deliverables already supplied, where the applicable terms and law permit the relevant treatment.

A general statement that a fee is "non-refundable" does not override statutory rights, remedies for a material service failure or other protections that apply by law. The actual circumstances must be assessed before a decision is made.

5. Education and admission consultancy services

Education guidance may involve research, counselling support, eligibility discussions, document review, application preparation or other agreed activities. The refund assessment may take account of which activities were actually completed and what the customer purchased.

MedOrbits cannot guarantee admission, a particular institution, a seat, a scholarship, a cutoff outcome or a decision by an admissions authority. An unsuccessful admission outcome alone does not automatically determine whether a consultancy fee is refundable; the agreed service and applicable law remain relevant.

Fees paid directly to a college, university, examination body, counselling authority, accommodation provider or another third party are generally governed by that organisation's own rules. MedOrbits cannot promise a third-party refund or represent that it can override the relevant institution's policy.

Customers should verify official admission rules, payment instructions, eligibility and deadlines with the relevant authority before making decisions.

6. Career, recruitment and related services

Career services may include resume assistance, application support, recruitment-related services, career guidance or other activities defined in a service agreement.

A refund review may consider the agreed deliverables, whether work has commenced, what has been supplied and any written conditions communicated before payment.

MedOrbits does not guarantee employment, an interview, a job offer, a particular salary or a hiring decision. An employer's independent decision does not by itself determine refund eligibility. However, this does not remove any rights a customer may have where a paid service was not supplied as agreed or applicable law provides a remedy.

7. Duplicate, excess and incorrect payments

If you believe that you have paid twice, paid more than the amount due or been charged incorrectly, contact MedOrbits with the relevant transaction details.

A review may involve checking payment references, invoice details, timestamps, the amount charged and confirmation from the payment provider. Where a duplicate or incorrect charge is verified, the appropriate correction or refund will be considered under the applicable terms and law.

A pending bank authorisation or temporary hold may not be the same as a completed charge. Your bank or payment provider may need to clarify whether the amount was actually debited, reversed or remains pending.

8. Cancellation and withdrawal requests

A request to cancel a service and a request for a refund are related but separate matters. Cancellation stops or seeks to stop future service activity; refund eligibility concerns whether money already paid should be returned.

If you wish to cancel a paid service, submit your request as soon as practicable and identify the service or transaction. The review may consider the service stage, work already completed, any commitments made to third parties, the written agreement and applicable law.

Review the Cancellation Policy for additional information. Do not assume that cancellation automatically creates a full refund or that a cancellation request can lawfully remove any rights you have under consumer law.

9. How to request a refund

Submit a refund request through the official MedOrbits contact page. Provide enough information for the relevant payment and service to be identified.

Include the following information where available

  • Your name and the contact details associated with the service.
  • The service name or a description of the service purchased.
  • The payment date and amount.
  • The invoice number, order reference or payment transaction reference.
  • A concise explanation of the reason for the request.
  • Relevant supporting evidence, with unnecessary sensitive information removed.

Do not send passwords, OTPs, banking PINs, full payment-card credentials or other authentication secrets. MedOrbits may request proportionate additional information when reasonably necessary to verify the transaction or prevent unauthorised refunds.

10. Review and decision process

After receiving a request, the relevant details should be reviewed against the transaction record, the service terms and any supporting evidence. Additional clarification may be requested if the information provided is incomplete or inconsistent.

The review may consider:

  • Whether the payment can be verified.
  • Which service was purchased and what terms applied.
  • Whether the service or relevant milestone was delivered.
  • Whether an error, duplicate charge or service issue occurred.
  • Whether a third party is responsible for the disputed amount.
  • Any mandatory legal requirements or applicable dispute procedures.

The outcome should be communicated through an appropriate contact channel where possible. If a request is declined in whole or in part, the explanation should identify the relevant reason, subject to any legal or security limitations.

11. Refund approval and processing time

When a refund is approved, the time for the funds to reach the original payment method may depend on the payment provider, bank, card issuer, transaction network and the method used to make the payment.

Any estimated processing timeframe should be confirmed for the specific transaction and payment method. This policy does not promise a universal number of days or guarantee when a bank or payment provider will post the credit.

If a refund appears delayed after confirmation, contact MedOrbits with the transaction reference and any refund confirmation available. You may also need to check with your bank or payment provider.

A refund approval and the actual posting of funds are separate stages. Keep the confirmation and transaction records until the matter is resolved.

12. Refund method and recipient verification

Where practicable and supported by the payment provider, an approved refund may be returned through the original payment method. This helps maintain a traceable connection between the original transaction and the refund.

If the original payment method is no longer available or a refund through that method cannot be completed, an alternative process may require additional verification and must comply with applicable law and payment-provider rules.

Refunds should not be redirected to an unrelated person or account without appropriate verification. MedOrbits should not ask a customer to disclose their OTP, banking PIN or payment-card security code to process an ordinary refund.

13. Partial refunds and service milestones

Depending on the agreement and circumstances, a partial refund may be considered where some parts of a service have been delivered while other agreed parts remain incomplete or have been cancelled.

A partial refund assessment may consider the work completed, deliverables supplied, any properly disclosed non-recoverable third-party expenses and the terms agreed before payment.

Any calculation should be based on the actual agreement and evidence, not an arbitrary deduction. Mandatory legal rights and remedies remain unaffected.

14. Payments made to third parties

If you paid an educational institution, examination body, government authority, employer, accommodation provider or another independent organisation directly, that organisation may control the refund decision.

You should consult the relevant organisation's official refund rules and contact its authorised payment or admissions office. MedOrbits cannot guarantee that a third party will approve or complete a refund.

If MedOrbits collected a payment on behalf of another organisation under an expressly agreed arrangement, the roles and applicable terms should be reviewed before deciding the request. Do not assume that every payment associated with an education or career process was paid to MedOrbits.

15. Failed, pending and reversed transactions

A payment failure, pending transaction, reversal and completed payment are different transaction states. A displayed error does not always mean that no money was debited.

If your account has been debited but the service or payment confirmation is missing, check your bank or payment-provider status and contact MedOrbits with the relevant reference. The transaction may need to be reconciled before the correct action can be determined.

If the bank or payment provider has already reversed the amount, provide the available confirmation so that duplicate reimbursement can be avoided.

16. Payment disputes and chargebacks

If you believe a charge is incorrect, you may contact MedOrbits to request a review. You may also have rights to raise a dispute with your bank, card issuer or payment provider under their procedures and applicable law.

Where a chargeback or payment dispute is already underway, the transaction may need to be handled through the relevant payment-provider process. Please provide any available dispute reference if you contact MedOrbits about the same payment.

A customer should not be discouraged from exercising lawful dispute rights. Likewise, duplicate reimbursement for the same transaction should be avoided through appropriate reconciliation.

17. Fraud prevention and suspicious requests

Refund requests may be checked for signs of impersonation, duplicate claims, altered payment evidence or other suspicious activity. Any verification should be proportionate to the risk and the information necessary to process the request.

MedOrbits should not request sensitive authentication secrets to verify a refund. Customers should use official communication channels and remain cautious of individuals demanding additional fees or promising guaranteed refunds outside the agreed process.

Report suspicious communications through the official contact page. You may also review the Anti-Fraud Policy.

18. Refund records and personal information

Information submitted with a refund request may be used to verify the payment, assess eligibility, communicate the decision, reconcile accounts and meet applicable legal or accounting requirements.

Access to refund records should be limited to authorised persons who need the information for an appropriate purpose. Records should be retained and disposed of in accordance with applicable requirements and the Privacy Policy and Data Retention Policy.

Please avoid including unrelated personal information in a refund request. If supporting documents are needed, provide only the information reasonably necessary for the review.

19. Exceptions and statutory rights

Some transactions may be governed by specific statutory rules, consumer protection requirements, payment-provider conditions or a separate written agreement. These requirements may affect whether a refund is due, the applicable process or the available remedies.

Nothing in this policy is intended to exclude, restrict or waive any right or remedy that cannot lawfully be excluded, restricted or waived. If a provision conflicts with a mandatory legal requirement, the applicable legal requirement will prevail to the extent of the conflict.

Where a customer believes that a decision overlooks an applicable right or relevant evidence, they may request clarification or use an available grievance or dispute resolution process.

20. Escalation and grievance redressal

If you disagree with the outcome of a refund review, contact MedOrbits through its official contact page and provide the original request reference, transaction details and the reason you believe the decision should be reconsidered.

Where applicable, you may also use the Grievance Redressal procedure or seek assistance from an appropriate consumer protection or dispute resolution authority.

The applicable external process depends on the nature of the transaction, the parties involved and the law governing the dispute.

21. Changes to this policy

This policy may be updated when MedOrbits' services, payment arrangements, refund procedures or legal obligations change. The latest published version should be consulted for current wording.

Changes to a policy do not automatically override the terms of an existing transaction or rights that have already arisen. The applicable agreement and law should be considered when determining which terms govern a particular payment.

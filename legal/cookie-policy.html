1. What are cookies?

Cookies are small text files that a website may store on your device when you visit it. They can help a site recognise a browser, maintain a session, remember selected preferences or support other website functions.

Cookies may be set by the website you visit, commonly called first-party cookies, or by another service integrated into the website, commonly called third-party cookies.

A cookie is not the only technology that can store or access information on a device. Depending on implementation, similar technologies may include local storage, session storage, pixels, tags, software development kits and scripts.

2. Why cookies may be used

Depending on the features enabled on MedOrbits, cookies or similar technologies may be used for purposes such as:

  • Supporting core website navigation and security.
  • Maintaining a session where a feature requires one.
  • Remembering language, accessibility or display preferences.
  • Understanding aggregate website performance and technical issues.
  • Supporting embedded content or external tools requested by a visitor.
  • Measuring campaign effectiveness or providing advertising, if those tools are deployed.
  • Remembering privacy preferences where a consent-management mechanism is implemented.

The purposes actually used must be confirmed against the live website and its third-party integrations. A technology should not be described as active unless it has been verified.

3. Types of cookies and similar technologies

Strictly necessary technologies

These may support essential functions such as security, load balancing, session handling or recording a consent choice. Depending on applicable law, some technologies may be used without consent when they are strictly necessary for a service specifically requested by the visitor. This is not a blanket exemption for every functional cookie.

Preference technologies

These may remember selected settings, such as a preferred language or other optional interface choices. Whether consent is required depends on the technology, its purpose and the law that applies.

Analytics technologies

These may collect information such as page views, referring pages, approximate usage patterns, browser characteristics and performance events. The data collected, retention period, recipients and consent requirements depend on the configured analytics provider and settings.

Advertising and third-party technologies

These may be introduced by advertising networks, video platforms, maps, social-media embeds or other external services. Such providers may collect information under their own practices. External integrations should be reviewed before they are activated on the site.

4. Session and persistent cookies

Session cookies are generally designed to expire when a browsing session ends. Persistent cookies remain on a device for a specified period or until they are deleted, depending on their settings and the browser.

Expiry periods differ by cookie and purpose. MedOrbits should publish specific durations only after verifying the actual cookie configuration and the retention practices of any relevant provider.

5. Your cookie choices

Your available choices depend on the technologies used, the features you access and the laws applicable to your visit. Where consent is required, optional technologies should not be activated before the relevant consent has been obtained.

If a cookie-preference banner or settings panel is provided on the live website, use it to review available categories, accept or reject optional categories, and change your choice later where the controls support that function.

This policy page does not itself store a preference or change the website's cookie settings. No consent-management interface is assumed to be installed by this document.

Consent controls must be implemented separately

If MedOrbits uses optional cookies or similar technologies, connect this page to a working preference interface and ensure the site's scripts respect the selected choices. A visible button alone is not sufficient if the underlying technologies continue to run contrary to the preference.

6. Managing cookies in your browser

Most modern browsers provide settings to inspect, restrict, block or delete cookies. The exact steps vary by browser, operating system and version.

  • Review the privacy or site-settings section in your browser.
  • Inspect cookies stored for the website, where your browser supports it.
  • Delete existing cookies or block future cookies according to your preferences.
  • Review private-browsing settings and any automatic deletion options.

Blocking all cookies may affect sign-in, session continuity, saved preferences or other site features. Deleting a cookie does not necessarily remove information already held by a service provider or stored on its servers.

Browser controls are separate from any consent interface provided by the website. Where applicable, use both to manage your choices.

7. Third-party services and embedded content

Some pages may link to or, if configured, embed services operated by third parties. When you interact with those services, the provider may process information under its own privacy and cookie practices.

Examples of possible integrations include video players, maps, social-media content, analytics services, payment providers and customer-support tools. These are examples only and do not confirm that any named category or provider is currently active on MedOrbits.

Review the provider's own documentation before using an external service. See the Third-Party Links Policy and Privacy Policy for related information.

8. Cookies and personal information

Depending on the technology and its configuration, information associated with cookies may relate to a device, browser, online activity or an identifiable person. The legal status of that information depends on the circumstances and applicable law.

The information collected, purpose of processing, recipients, storage periods and applicable rights should be explained in the Privacy Policy and relevant notices for the actual services used.

Do not assume that cookie data is anonymous merely because it does not directly display a person's name. Online identifiers and usage data can sometimes be combined with other information.

9. Consent and applicable law

Cookie and tracking requirements vary by jurisdiction, technology and purpose. Certain technologies may require prior consent, while narrowly defined essential uses may be treated differently under applicable rules.

Where consent is required, it should be requested through an appropriate mechanism before the relevant optional technologies operate. Visitors should be able to make a genuine choice, and any withdrawal mechanism should work as required by applicable law.

This policy is general information and does not determine which legal regime applies to every visitor or technology. MedOrbits should assess its actual audience, services, providers and data flows before finalising its compliance arrangements.

10. Retention and cookie inventory

Cookie duration depends on the specific cookie, its purpose and the settings applied by the website or provider. Some identifiers may be deleted at the end of a session, while others may persist for a configured period.

A complete cookie inventory should identify, where applicable:

  • Cookie or technology name.
  • Provider or domain setting it.
  • Purpose and category.
  • Duration or expiry.
  • Whether it is first-party or third-party.
  • Whether consent is required and how the technology responds to user choices.

This inventory must be generated from a review of the live website, including relevant subdomains, embedded content and scripts. Do not treat the category cards on this page as a verified cookie scan.

11. Security and changes to this policy

Website technologies should be configured and maintained with appropriate security controls. Third-party integrations should be reviewed for their purpose, data access and continued necessity.

MedOrbits may update this policy when website functionality, service providers, legal requirements or data practices change. The published page should be reviewed whenever a material change is made to cookies or similar technologies.

Where required, material changes to consent practices should be accompanied by appropriate notices and a renewed consent process.

12. Questions about cookies or privacy

For questions about this policy or privacy-related requests, visit the MedOrbits contact page and consult the Privacy Policy.

Do not send passwords, one-time passcodes, payment-card security codes or unnecessary sensitive personal documents in a general enquiry.

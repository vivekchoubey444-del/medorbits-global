Accessibility for everyone.

MedOrbits aims to make education resources, admission guidance, career information and digital services usable by as many people as possible, including people with disabilities. This statement explains our accessibility approach, practical recommendations, known limitations and how to request assistance or report a barrier.

01

Purpose and Scope

Where this statement applies

This Accessibility Statement describes the approach MedOrbits takes to improving access to its website at https://medorbits.in/ and to the digital content and services made available through it.

The statement is intended for students, applicants, job seekers, employers, visitors and other users who may encounter barriers because of visual, auditory, motor, cognitive, neurological or other accessibility needs.

It covers website navigation, written content, forms, application workflows, downloadable documents and relevant interactive features, to the extent those elements are operated or controlled by MedOrbits.

Accessibility can vary between pages, devices, browsers, assistive technologies and third-party services. This statement should be read as an explanation of our approach, not a guarantee that every experience is barrier-free.

02

Accessibility Commitment

Designing for different users and needs

MedOrbits aims to make its digital information easier to perceive, understand and operate. We recognize that people use different devices, input methods and assistive technologies to access online information.

Our intended accessibility practices include:

  • Using meaningful headings and a logical content hierarchy.
  • Providing keyboard-accessible controls where feasible.
  • Maintaining visible focus indicators for interactive elements.
  • Using readable text, suitable spacing and responsive layouts.
  • Providing meaningful alternative text for informative images where appropriate.
  • Labelling forms and communicating errors as clearly as possible.
  • Considering reduced-motion preferences in supported interfaces.
  • Reviewing accessibility reports and prioritizing practical improvements.

These are design objectives and implementation practices, not a representation that every listed feature is already present or works perfectly on every page.

03

Guidelines and Standards

Accessibility principles we consider

Our accessibility work may use the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), published by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C), as a reference for identifying and addressing common website accessibility barriers.

WCAG organizes accessibility around four principles: content should be perceivable, operable, understandable and robust.

  • Perceivable: users should be able to access information through the senses available to them.
  • Operable: interactive features should be usable through supported input methods.
  • Understandable: information, navigation and controls should be as clear and predictable as possible.
  • Robust: content should work with a reasonable range of browsers and assistive technologies.

Reference: W3C Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2.

Unless an accessibility audit confirms the relevant criteria, MedOrbits does not claim formal certification or full conformance to WCAG or any other technical standard.

04

Readable and Perceivable Content

Making information easier to access

We aim to present website information in a structured, readable format. Page content should use clear headings, descriptive link labels and meaningful grouping wherever appropriate.

  • Use headings to communicate the topic and hierarchy of a section.
  • Write link text that explains its destination or purpose.
  • Keep paragraphs and instructions understandable.
  • Avoid relying on colour alone to communicate important information.
  • Use lists for grouped requirements, steps and related information.
  • Provide text alternatives for meaningful visual information where appropriate.

Some content may originate from external institutions, employers, publishers or other providers. Its structure and accessibility may be outside our direct control.

05

Keyboard Navigation

Using the website without a mouse

We aim to support keyboard access for important navigation and interactive controls. Users who cannot operate a mouse should be able to use standard keyboard commands wherever the relevant feature supports them.

Common keyboard commands

  • Tab: move forward between focusable controls.
  • Shift + Tab: move backward between focusable controls.
  • Enter: activate a focused link or button.
  • Space: activate supported buttons and controls.
  • Escape: close supported menus or overlays.

The exact behaviour depends on the browser, operating system and component. If a keyboard trap, inaccessible control or unexpected focus movement prevents you from completing a task, please report the affected page and the steps that reproduce the issue.

06

Screen Readers and Assistive Technology

Supporting alternative ways to browse

People may access MedOrbits using screen readers, screen magnifiers, speech recognition, switch devices, alternative keyboards and other assistive technologies.

Where practical, pages should use semantic HTML elements, meaningful document titles, descriptive accessible names and labels that help assistive technology communicate the purpose of a control.

  • Use headings to make sections easier to navigate.
  • Provide labels for form fields and useful descriptions for errors.
  • Identify navigation and main content regions where appropriate.
  • Use descriptive text for links and buttons rather than ambiguous labels.
  • Ensure important instructions are available as text, not only as images.

Assistive technology support can differ between browsers and versions. We welcome reports about compatibility problems involving specific screen readers or other tools.

07

Visual Design and Readability

Contrast, scaling and visual comfort

MedOrbits uses a dark burgundy and emerald visual theme with contrasting text and accent colours. We aim to preserve sufficient readability across screen sizes and supported display settings.

  • Use text and background combinations intended to remain distinguishable.
  • Provide visible focus styles for interactive elements.
  • Use responsive layouts that adapt to different viewport widths.
  • Avoid unnecessarily long lines and overly dense content blocks where practical.
  • Respect reduced-motion preferences for supported animated or transitioning elements.
  • Use more than colour alone to distinguish important states or instructions.

Browser zoom, operating-system scaling and user-defined contrast settings may affect the appearance of some elements. If text becomes clipped, overlaps other content or becomes unreadable when enlarged, please let us know.

08

Navigation and Page Structure

Predictable browsing and clear page landmarks

We aim to use consistent navigation, meaningful page titles, logical content order and clear labels to help users understand where they are and how to find relevant information.

  • Provide a consistent route to major website sections.
  • Use breadcrumbs or equivalent navigation cues on supported pages.
  • Make important page sections easy to identify.
  • Provide a skip link on pages where it is implemented.
  • Keep mobile navigation usable on smaller screens.
  • Ensure navigation controls communicate their expanded or collapsed state where applicable.

The location, labels and availability of navigation items may vary by page or service. If a menu cannot be opened, closed or navigated using your preferred input method, report the issue through our contact page.

09

Forms and Validation

Applications, contact forms and data entry

Forms may be used to request guidance, submit applications, contact support or provide information for education and career services. We aim to make field labels, instructions and required information understandable.

  • Clearly identify the purpose of each form field.
  • Indicate required fields and explain applicable formats.
  • Provide useful error messages when validation fails.
  • Avoid relying only on colour to indicate an error.
  • Keep instructions available while a user completes a task where practical.
  • Provide a way to review information before final submission when the workflow supports it.

Some forms may use third-party tools or document-upload services. Their accessibility and technical requirements may vary. If a form prevents you from completing a legitimate request, contact us and identify the form and the step where the problem occurs.

Privacy reminder: When asking for assistance, do not send passwords, one-time codes, payment-card security codes or unnecessary sensitive documents through an ordinary support message.
10

Images and Non-Text Content

Alternative ways to understand visual information

Informative images should have meaningful text alternatives where appropriate. Decorative images should not create unnecessary noise for assistive technology.

  • Provide alternative text for images that convey information.
  • Use descriptive names for image-based controls where needed.
  • Avoid placing essential instructions only inside an image.
  • Use captions or nearby explanatory text for complex diagrams when appropriate.
  • Ensure logos and linked images communicate their purpose.

Some third-party images, legacy graphics or uploaded documents may not have adequate text alternatives. If an image contains essential information that is unavailable to you, tell us which page it appears on and what information you need to access.

11

Audio and Video

Accessing multimedia content

Where MedOrbits provides or embeds audio and video content, we aim to consider alternatives that make important information available to users with different sensory needs.

  • Provide captions for spoken content in video where available and appropriate.
  • Consider transcripts for audio-only material.
  • Avoid autoplaying media with unexpected sound where practical.
  • Make playback controls accessible using supported input methods.
  • Consider descriptions or explanatory text when visual details convey essential information.

Media hosted by third parties may have different controls, captions or accessibility features. Availability may depend on the original publisher and the media platform.

If a video or audio item contains essential information that you cannot access, contact us to describe the item and request an accessible alternative where reasonably available.

12

Documents and Downloads

PDFs, forms and educational resources

MedOrbits may provide downloadable documents, application forms, guides, brochures and educational resources. The accessibility of a file can depend on how it was created, scanned, exported or supplied by its original publisher.

  • Prefer searchable text documents over image-only scans when feasible.
  • Use meaningful document titles and structured headings where supported.
  • Use descriptive link text to identify downloads.
  • Consider accessible alternatives when a document cannot be read with assistive technology.
  • Identify the exact file when requesting help or an alternative format.

Some official forms and institutional documents may be published in formats that MedOrbits does not control. We cannot guarantee that every external document is accessible or editable.

If a document is essential to a service and inaccessible to you, contact us. Where reasonably possible, we will consider an alternative way to communicate the relevant information or assist with access.

13

Browser and Device Compatibility

Access across supported devices

MedOrbits is designed to be responsive across a range of modern browsers and devices. The experience may differ depending on browser version, operating system, screen size, connection quality and assistive technology.

For the best available experience, consider using a supported, up-to-date browser and enabling the accessibility features available on your device.

  • Browser zoom and text-size settings.
  • Screen readers and spoken-content features.
  • Operating-system contrast and colour adjustments.
  • Keyboard navigation and alternative input devices.
  • Reduced-motion settings where supported.

These suggestions are optional and do not replace our responsibility to consider accessibility in the website design. If a particular browser or device causes a barrier, please include its name and version when reporting the issue, if you can identify them.

14

Third-Party Content and Services

External platforms and linked resources

MedOrbits may link to or embed content from colleges, universities, examination authorities, government portals, employers, payment providers, video platforms and other external services.

We do not control every design decision or technical feature on these external services. Their accessibility may differ from the MedOrbits website.

  • External websites may use different navigation and login methods.
  • Third-party documents may not include accessible text or structure.
  • Embedded content may depend on external scripts or browser settings.
  • External accessibility features may change without notice to MedOrbits.

If a third-party service is inaccessible, consider contacting that provider directly. You may also inform MedOrbits if the external resource is essential to a MedOrbits-related task, so we can consider whether an alternative source or route is available.

15

Known Limitations

What may still create accessibility barriers

Accessibility is an ongoing process, and some parts of the website may not yet provide the experience we intend. Potential limitations may include:

  • Older pages or documents that have not been reviewed recently.
  • Scanned PDFs or third-party files without selectable text.
  • External forms, embedded services or media players.
  • Complex tables, graphics or content supplied by external organizations.
  • Occasional issues with focus order, field descriptions or responsive layouts.
  • Differences between assistive technology and browser combinations.

This list describes possible types of limitations and is not a verified audit of current defects. The exact barriers present on the live website should be established through testing and user feedback.

We encourage users to report specific issues so they can be assessed, prioritized and addressed where feasible.

16

Testing and Improvements

How accessibility can be improved over time

Accessibility improvements may include automated checks, manual review, keyboard testing, responsive testing and feedback from people who use assistive technologies.

Improvement work may consider:

  • Document structure, heading hierarchy and page landmarks.
  • Accessible names, labels and descriptions for controls.
  • Keyboard access, focus order and visible focus indicators.
  • Text contrast, zoom behaviour and responsive reflow.
  • Form validation, error communication and submission status.
  • Alternative text, captions and accessible document formats.

Automated testing can identify some technical problems but cannot establish accessibility on its own. Manual review and feedback from users remain important.

We may prioritize issues based on their effect on essential tasks, the number of users affected, available alternatives, technical feasibility and applicable legal obligations.

17

Alternative Access and Assistance

When a feature is difficult to use

If an accessibility barrier prevents you from accessing important information or completing a legitimate task, contact MedOrbits and explain what you need to access.

Depending on the request and available resources, we may consider reasonable assistance such as:

  • Providing the relevant information in an alternative text format.
  • Explaining a process or instruction in clearer language.
  • Helping identify an accessible version of a document.
  • Providing guidance on how to locate a page or feature.
  • Reviewing a form or workflow that cannot be completed with your input method.

The assistance available will depend on the nature of the request, the material involved, applicable law and whether the content is controlled by MedOrbits or a third party.

Helpful details: Tell us which page or file you are using, what you were trying to do, what happened, and which format or assistance would help you. You do not need to disclose a diagnosis or unnecessary medical information.
18

Report an Accessibility Barrier

How to share feedback or request support

We welcome feedback about barriers that make the MedOrbits website difficult to perceive, navigate or use. Reports can help us understand problems that may not be identified through routine testing.

What to include in your report

  • The page URL or name of the affected document.
  • A short description of the accessibility problem.
  • The action you were trying to complete.
  • The steps that caused the issue, if you can describe them.
  • Your browser, device or assistive technology details, if known.
  • The alternative format or assistance you would prefer.

Protect your privacy

Please do not send passwords, one-time codes, financial credentials or unnecessary sensitive documents. You do not need to provide medical details to report an accessibility issue.

Contacting us does not guarantee a specific response time or resolution. We will consider the information provided and assess the issue according to its nature and priority.

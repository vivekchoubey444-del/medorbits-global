Data Protection.

This policy explains the principles MedOrbits applies when handling personal information in connection with its website, education resources, admission guidance, career information and related services. It describes data collection, use, sharing, retention, security and ways to raise a data-protection concern.

This policy describes general data-protection principles and must be read with the published Privacy Policy and applicable service terms.

1. Purpose and scope

This Data Protection Policy sets out a framework for responsible handling of personal information associated with MedOrbits and its services. It is intended to help users understand the principal safeguards and responsibilities relevant to information they provide or generate while interacting with the website.

It may apply to information submitted through contact forms, guidance requests, application-preparation workflows, document submissions, service enquiries and other supported interactions. The actual processing performed depends on the features available and the particular service requested.

This policy complements the Privacy Policy. If a particular service has a separate privacy notice, contractual term or legally required disclosure, review that document as well.

2. Data-protection principles

Personal information should be handled in a way that is appropriate to the purpose for which it is collected and consistent with applicable law. The following principles guide the intended approach:

  • Purpose limitation: collect and use information for stated, legitimate purposes.
  • Data minimisation: avoid requesting information that is unnecessary for the relevant task.
  • Accuracy: provide suitable ways to correct information where supported and applicable.
  • Security: use appropriate safeguards proportionate to the type and risk of information handled.
  • Limited retention: keep information only as long as justified by the relevant purpose, legal obligations or legitimate operational needs.
  • Accountability: review data-handling practices and applicable requirements as services evolve.
  • Transparency: explain material data practices through the relevant privacy notices and service information.

These principles describe the intended framework; they do not imply that every technical or organisational control mentioned in a general description is already implemented in every system or workflow.

3. Categories of information

Depending on the service and information you choose to provide, relevant categories may include:

Identity and contact details

Name, email address, telephone number and other contact details submitted through a form or service interaction.

Education and career information

Educational qualifications, course preferences, institution preferences, examination-related details, work history, resume information and career interests where relevant to the requested guidance.

Application and supporting documents

Information contained in documents that you voluntarily submit for an applicable service. Such documents may include educational records or identity-related information. Submit these only when necessary and through an appropriate channel.

Service and transaction information

Enquiry history, application references, service status, payment references and records necessary to administer a requested service or respond to a dispute.

Technical and usage information

Depending on the website configuration, limited technical information may be processed, such as browser details, device information, diagnostic events, security logs and website interaction data. Actual collection depends on deployed features and settings.

4. How information may be collected

Information may be obtained directly from you when you submit a form, contact support, request guidance, provide documents, create or use an account where available, or otherwise interact with a MedOrbits service.

Some information may be generated through service delivery, transaction administration, website operations or security monitoring. Where a third-party service is involved, that provider may process information under its own applicable terms and privacy notice.

Publicly available institutional or official information may be used to prepare education and career resources. Information about an institution or course is not necessarily personal information about an individual.

Information should not be collected through deceptive practices or by requesting credentials that are not needed for the service.

5. Why information may be used

Depending on the interaction, personal information may be processed for purposes such as:

  • Responding to enquiries and providing requested guidance.
  • Preparing, administering or following up on a service request.
  • Organising application-related information and supporting documents when the service requires it.
  • Communicating service updates, clarifications or material changes.
  • Processing or reconciling payments through applicable payment providers.
  • Maintaining records necessary for accounting, dispute resolution or legal compliance.
  • Protecting systems and users against suspected fraud, abuse, unauthorised access or other security incidents.
  • Diagnosing errors and improving website usability, where relevant and permitted.
  • Meeting applicable legal obligations or responding to lawful requests.

Personal information should not be repurposed in a materially incompatible way without an appropriate basis, notice or permission where required by law.

6. Legal basis and permission

Where applicable law requires a legal basis for processing, the relevant basis may depend on the circumstances. This may include your consent, steps requested by you before entering into a contract, performance of a contract, compliance with a legal obligation or another basis recognised by applicable law.

Where consent is required, it should be requested in an appropriate manner. If processing relies on consent, you may be able to withdraw it, subject to applicable law and the consequences explained at the time of the request.

Withdrawing consent does not necessarily invalidate processing lawfully carried out before withdrawal, and some information may need to be retained for legal, accounting, security or dispute-resolution purposes.

The specific basis for a particular processing activity should be described in the applicable privacy notice or service documentation where required.

7. Sensitive and high-risk information

Educational records, identity documents, financial references and other information can create elevated risks if disclosed or misused. Some categories may also receive special protection under applicable law.

Before submitting high-risk information:

  • Confirm that the requested information is necessary for the service.
  • Verify that you are using an official and appropriate submission channel.
  • Check whether an alternative document or redacted copy is acceptable.
  • Remove unrelated information where it is not needed and redaction is permitted.
  • Do not send account passwords, OTPs, banking PINs or card security codes.
  • Keep a record of what you submitted and why, where appropriate.

Do not upload another person's personal documents unless you have the authority and lawful permission required to provide them.

8. Sharing and disclosure

Personal information may need to be disclosed to an appropriate recipient to provide a requested service or meet a lawful obligation. Depending on the circumstances, recipients may include:

  • Service providers supporting website hosting, communications, document handling or technical operations.
  • Payment service providers handling a transaction.
  • Educational institutions, employers or other organisations when you request a service that requires sharing information with them.
  • Professional advisers or authorised representatives where necessary for legitimate business or legal purposes.
  • Competent public authorities when disclosure is legally required or otherwise lawfully permitted.

Information should be shared only to the extent appropriate for the relevant purpose, subject to applicable law and any required permission.

A link to an external website does not establish that the external organisation is controlled by or affiliated with MedOrbits. Review the external provider's privacy terms before submitting information.

See the Third-Party Links Policy for related information.

9. Third-party service providers

Some website functions may rely on external service providers. Their involvement and the information they process depend on which features are enabled and used.

Where a provider processes personal information on behalf of the business, appropriate contractual, organisational or other safeguards should be considered as required by applicable law and the nature of the service.

Third-party providers may separately process information under their own terms when acting as independent service providers or controllers. MedOrbits cannot represent that every third-party system operates under identical security, retention or privacy practices.

10. Data security safeguards

Appropriate technical and organisational safeguards should be used to reduce the risk of unauthorised access, alteration, disclosure, loss or destruction of personal information. Safeguards should reflect the sensitivity of the information, the systems involved and the risks associated with processing.

Depending on the relevant system, suitable measures may include:

  • Restricting access to people who need information for an authorised purpose.
  • Using secure transmission and storage configurations where supported.
  • Applying appropriate authentication and access-management controls.
  • Maintaining relevant system updates and security configurations.
  • Using suitable backups and recovery arrangements where required.
  • Reviewing access and security events where appropriate.
  • Providing processes for handling suspected data-security incidents.

These are examples of safeguards, not a guarantee that every measure is deployed in every MedOrbits system. No internet transmission or electronic storage method can be guaranteed to be completely secure.

Your role in protecting data

Use trusted devices, keep your accounts secure, verify the destination before uploading documents and report suspected unauthorised access promptly.

11. Data retention and deletion

Personal information should be retained only for as long as reasonably necessary for the purposes for which it was collected, unless a longer period is required or permitted by applicable law.

Retention may depend on factors such as:

  • The duration of the requested service or user relationship.
  • Legal, regulatory, accounting or record-keeping requirements.
  • The need to resolve complaints, payment disputes or legal claims.
  • Security, fraud-prevention and audit requirements.
  • Whether information can be deleted, anonymised or otherwise safely disposed of.

Retention periods may differ across records and systems. Do not assume that closing an account, completing an application or withdrawing consent automatically deletes every related record.

Where deletion is requested, the request should be assessed under applicable law and any relevant retention obligations. Information that is no longer required should be securely deleted or otherwise disposed of when appropriate.

12. Your data-protection rights

Depending on applicable law, your relationship with the service and the circumstances of processing, you may have rights concerning your personal information. These may include rights to:

  • Receive information about relevant data-handling practices.
  • Request access to personal information where a right of access applies.
  • Request correction or updating of inaccurate information.
  • Request deletion or erasure where legally available.
  • Withdraw consent where processing relies on consent.
  • Raise an objection or request restriction where such rights apply.
  • Request a copy or transfer of information where applicable law provides that right.
  • Raise a complaint with a competent authority where available.

These rights are not absolute in every situation. Legal exceptions, verification requirements and third-party rights may affect how a request can be handled.

To raise a request, use the official contact page and identify the relevant record or service where possible. Do not send more identity information than is reasonably necessary to verify the request.

13. Handling data requests

When a data-related request is received, the business should take appropriate steps to understand its scope, verify the requester's authority where necessary and determine the applicable legal requirements.

A request may require clarification if it does not identify the relevant information or service. A request may also need to be limited, delayed or declined where applicable law permits, such as where it would disclose another person's information or conflict with a valid retention obligation.

Any response timeframe should follow applicable law and any confirmed service commitment. This policy does not promise a particular processing period where one has not been established.

14. Children and young users

Education-related services may be relevant to students who are under the age of majority. Where personal information relating to a child is involved, processing must follow applicable legal requirements, including any required parental or guardian consent and additional safeguards.

A parent, guardian or authorised representative should use the official contact route for questions about a child's information and should provide only information necessary to address the request.

Do not assume that a general enquiry or document-upload facility is automatically appropriate for every category of child-related information. Specific service requirements and applicable law must be considered.

15. Data location and international transfers

Depending on the technology and service providers used, personal information may be processed or stored in locations different from where the user is located. Actual storage and transfer arrangements depend on the systems deployed.

Where a cross-border transfer is subject to legal restrictions, the applicable requirements and safeguards must be considered before the transfer takes place. Relevant transfer information should be described in the Privacy Policy or a service-specific notice where required.

This policy does not assert a specific server location or guarantee that all data remains within a particular country.

16. Data breaches and security incidents

A suspected data breach or security incident should be assessed promptly using an appropriate incident-handling process. Depending on the circumstances, relevant actions may include containing the incident, investigating its scope, preserving evidence, restoring affected systems and taking steps to reduce further harm.

Notifications to affected individuals, regulators or other parties should be made when required by applicable law and within the applicable timeframe.

If you believe that your personal information has been exposed, sent to the wrong recipient or accessed without permission, report the concern through the official contact page. Include a concise description and relevant dates, but do not send passwords or OTPs.

Reporting a concern does not itself confirm that a breach has occurred. Any investigation must establish the facts.

17. Cookies, analytics and technical data

The website may use cookies or similar technologies if those features are configured. Such technologies can support essential website functions, preferences, security, performance measurement or other disclosed purposes.

Actual cookie and analytics practices should be described in the applicable cookie notice or Privacy Policy. Browser settings may allow some cookies to be controlled, but blocking certain technologies can affect website functionality.

This page does not claim that a specific analytics tool, advertising tracker or cookie category is active. The live website configuration should be checked before publishing a definitive list of technologies.

18. Accuracy and user responsibilities

Users should provide information that is accurate to the best of their knowledge and update it when necessary for an ongoing service.

Do not submit falsified certificates, altered identity records, another person's documents without authority or information obtained through unlawful means. Incorrect or misleading information may affect the service and could have legal or institutional consequences.

If you discover that information you supplied is incorrect, use the official contact route to request a correction. Include the relevant reference and describe the correction needed.

19. Fraud prevention and data misuse

Personal information should not be used to impersonate another person, make unauthorised applications, deceive institutions, access another user's account or conduct fraudulent transactions.

MedOrbits may take reasonable and lawful steps to review suspected misuse, protect systems, preserve relevant records and respond to lawful requests. Any action depends on the available facts, applicable law and the capabilities of the systems involved.

If you receive a suspicious request for documents, payment or account credentials from someone claiming to represent MedOrbits, verify the request through an independently accessed official channel.

Read the Anti-Fraud Policy for further guidance.

20. Accountability and policy review

Data-protection practices should be reviewed as services, technical systems, processing purposes and legal requirements change. Responsibility for implementation should be assigned within the operating business.

This policy describes a general framework. It does not establish a specific internal team, certification, audit schedule or technical control unless that detail is independently confirmed and documented.

Material changes should be reflected in the published policy, with an updated revision date where appropriate. Where applicable law requires additional notice or consent, those requirements should be followed.

21. Questions and complaints

If you have a question about how your information is handled, or wish to raise a data-protection concern, use the MedOrbits contact page and clearly identify the nature of your request.

For a formal service complaint, consult the Grievance Redressal procedure. Any legally required privacy contact or grievance officer details should be published after verification and updated when they change.

Where applicable law gives you the right to complain to a regulator or other competent authority, this policy does not remove or replace that right.

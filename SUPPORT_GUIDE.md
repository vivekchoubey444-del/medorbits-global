MedOrbits Support Guide

MedOrbits — Education · Admissions · Careers · Recruitment

1. Purpose

This guide explains how users can prepare, submit, and follow up on support requests concerning MedOrbits services, website functionality, educational resources, admissions guidance, career resources, and application-related workflows.

Our support process is intended to encourage clear communication, responsible handling of information, and efficient investigation of reported issues.

2. Official Support Channels

Use only contact methods and support channels that MedOrbits has officially published and verified.

Before publishing or relying on a support address, telephone number, contact form, security reporting address, or policy link, confirm that it is active, controlled by the intended organization, and appropriate for the relevant purpose.

Do not assume that a general contact channel is suitable for confidential documents, account recovery, payment disputes, or vulnerability reports.

If a channel cannot be verified, do not represent it as an official or guaranteed support route.

3. Information to Include in a Support Request

Provide only the information reasonably necessary to understand and investigate your issue.

Where applicable, include:

* Affected service: The relevant MedOrbits page, feature, application workflow, or resource.
* Issue summary: A concise explanation of the problem and its impact.
* Expected behaviour: What you expected the service to do.
* Actual behaviour: What happened instead, including any displayed error message.
* Reproduction steps: A numbered sequence explaining how the issue occurred.
* Approximate date and time: Include the time zone where relevant.
* Environment: Browser, device type, operating system, or relevant application version.
* Sanitized evidence: Redacted screenshots, error messages, or logs that do not expose confidential information.
* Reference number: An existing application, transaction, or support reference, where appropriate and safe to share.

Do not include unrelated personal information or attach entire documents when a limited excerpt or redacted screenshot is sufficient.

4. Protect Your Information

Never send the following through ordinary support messages:

* Passwords, one-time passwords (OTPs), recovery codes, or security answers.
* API keys, access tokens, session cookies, private keys, or authentication credentials.
* Full payment-card numbers, card verification codes, or banking credentials.
* Unnecessary identity documents, medical records, financial records, or other sensitive applicant information.
* Unredacted server logs, database exports, or files containing other users’ information.

If a legitimate application workflow requires supporting documents, use only the relevant, verified submission process and provide the minimum information required.

Before sharing evidence, remove or obscure credentials, personal identifiers, document numbers, private URLs, payment information, and information belonging to other people.

Security principle: MedOrbits support should not require you to disclose your password or OTP to a support representative.

5. Application and Admission-Related Issues

For issues involving admissions guidance, application preparation, document checklists, or application status, identify the relevant service and describe the specific difficulty.

Where available and appropriate, provide a reference number rather than repeating an applicant’s personal information.

Please distinguish between:

* A MedOrbits website or technical issue.
* A question about published educational information.
* A request for application-preparation assistance.
* A decision, deadline, eligibility requirement, or status controlled by an external institution or official authority.

MedOrbits cannot independently guarantee admission, seat allotment, eligibility, document acceptance, or an outcome controlled by a college, examination authority, counselling body, employer, or government agency.

Verify consequential requirements, deadlines, fees, and decisions against the applicable official source.

6. Website Errors and Technical Problems

When reporting a technical problem, explain the steps that lead to it and whether it occurs consistently.

Useful evidence may include:

1. The affected page or feature.
2. The approximate time of the incident.
3. The action that triggered the problem.
4. The visible error message or sanitized screenshot.
5. The browser and device used.
6. Whether refreshing the page or repeating the action changes the result.

Do not repeatedly submit an application or make a payment merely to reproduce an error. First check whether the original action succeeded, and use the relevant verified transaction or application reference when requesting assistance.

7. Privacy and Data Handling

Share information only through an appropriate, verified channel and only when necessary for the requested assistance.

Do not assume that sending information to a support channel makes it confidential, guarantees deletion, or establishes a particular retention period.

The applicable privacy notice and terms should explain the relevant data-handling practices. Verify these documents and their links before publication.

If you believe your personal information has been exposed, accessed without authorization, or sent to the wrong recipient, report the incident through an appropriate verified private channel. Avoid posting the affected information publicly.

8. Security Vulnerability Reporting

Suspected security vulnerabilities must be reported through the verified private process documented in SECURITY.md.

Do not disclose vulnerability details in public issues, public discussions, social media posts, or other channels that could expose users or systems to harm.

A responsible report should include, where safe and appropriate:

* The affected component or page.
* A concise description of the suspected vulnerability.
* Reproduction steps limited to authorized testing.
* The potential impact.
* Sanitized evidence and relevant timestamps.
* Any practical mitigation information already identified.

Do not access other users’ accounts or data, extract confidential information, disrupt services, or conduct testing beyond your authorization.

Do not include live credentials, tokens, private keys, or sensitive user data in the report.

Security reports should be handled through the process and contact details actually established in SECURITY.md. Do not invent a security email address or promise a response time, reward, or resolution deadline that has not been formally approved and published.

9. Reference Numbers and Follow-Up

If a support request or transaction has an existing reference number, retain it and use it for relevant follow-up messages.

Avoid creating multiple duplicate reports for the same issue unless the original reporting channel is unavailable or a new, material development requires a separate report.

Do not assume that a request has been received, assigned, resolved, or escalated unless the relevant channel provides confirmation.

Any published response targets, service hours, escalation routes, or service-level commitments must reflect an approved and operational support policy.

10. External Services and Official Sources

Some information or processes referenced by MedOrbits may be managed by third-party institutions, service providers, examination authorities, counselling bodies, colleges, or employers.

For matters controlled by those organizations, consult their verified official websites and published contact channels.

Before publishing a link to a policy, institution, application portal, or external support service, verify that the destination is correct, relevant, and current.

A third-party website or communication should not be represented as an official MedOrbits channel.

11. Accessibility and Clear Communication

When submitting a request, use concise language and describe the issue in the language most appropriate for the support channel, where supported.

If a problem affects accessibility, mobile usability, form navigation, document readability, or language rendering, identify the affected page and explain the barrier encountered.

Do not include unnecessary personal details to explain an accessibility issue.

12. Scope and Limitations

This guide describes recommended support-reporting practices. It does not independently establish:

* Guaranteed response or resolution times.
* Published support hours or 24-hour availability.
* Guaranteed escalation or priority handling.
* Admission, recruitment, financial, or application outcomes.
* Security disclosure rewards or response commitments.
* Privacy, retention, or deletion guarantees beyond the applicable published policies.

Such commitments may be stated only when supported by verified, approved, and operational policies.

13. Maintenance and Publication

Before publishing this guide:

* Verify the official support channels.
* Review SECURITY.md and confirm that its private reporting process is operational.
* Verify the applicable privacy notice and terms links.
* Confirm that referenced pages and services exist.
* Remove unsupported response targets, service hours, or guarantees.
* Review the document whenever relevant policies or support procedures change.

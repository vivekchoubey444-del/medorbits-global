1. Purpose and Commitment

MedOrbits is committed to maintaining a professional, respectful, inclusive and responsible environment for everyone who contributes to, maintains, reviews, uses or represents the project.

Our work spans education, admissions, career guidance, recruitment, application services, software development and informational resources. These areas require professional communication, responsible conduct, respect for individual dignity and appropriate protection of information.

This Code of Conduct establishes the behavioral standards expected in MedOrbits project spaces and in interactions where individuals officially represent the project.

We recognize that constructive disagreement, rigorous technical review, honest criticism and diverse perspectives strengthen a project when expressed responsibly.

Every participant shares responsibility for fostering an environment free from harassment, discrimination, intimidation, retaliation and abuse of authority.

Our commitment is to treat people fairly, protect their dignity, act responsibly and address misconduct consistently.

2. Scope and Applicability

This policy applies to participation in MedOrbits project activities and interactions, including:

* Source-code repositories, commits, pull requests and code reviews.
* Issue trackers, technical discussions and project documentation.
* Project-related chat rooms, mailing lists and collaboration platforms.
* Technical reviews, planning sessions, meetings and workshops organized by MedOrbits.
* Online events and in-person activities organized or officially represented by the project.
* Communications through official project accounts or communications made while representing MedOrbits.
* Interactions involving maintainers, contributors, reviewers, contractors, volunteers and other authorized participants in covered project spaces.

This policy may also apply to off-platform conduct when a clear connection exists to project participation, official representation, participant safety or the integrity of project activities.

It is not intended to regulate unrelated private conduct merely because an individual participates in MedOrbits.

Separate website terms, privacy notices, employment policies, service agreements, platform rules and applicable laws may impose additional obligations. This policy does not replace those requirements.

3. Community Standards

All participants are expected to uphold the following standards.

3.1 Respect and Professionalism

Participants must:

* Communicate courteously, including during disagreements.
* Critique ideas, implementations and decisions rather than attacking individuals.
* Avoid insults, humiliation, intimidation and deliberately hostile communication.
* Respect reasonable personal boundaries and requests to discontinue unwanted contact.
* Recognize the time, expertise and effort contributed by others.
* Use professional language appropriate to the context and audience.

3.2 Inclusion and Equal Treatment

MedOrbits does not tolerate harassment or discriminatory treatment based on characteristics protected by applicable law.

Participants must not demean, threaten, exclude or disadvantage others on grounds such as race, caste, ethnicity, nationality, religion, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, age or socioeconomic background.

Differences in education, experience, technical ability, language, communication style or professional seniority must not be used to justify mistreatment.

3.3 Constructive Collaboration

Participants are expected to:

* Provide clear, relevant and actionable feedback.
* Accept reasonable criticism without personal retaliation.
* Explain technical concerns using evidence, reproducible examples and documented reasoning where practical.
* Acknowledge mistakes and correct them responsibly.
* Follow agreed review procedures and project workflows.
* Raise disagreements through appropriate channels without intimidation or personal attacks.

Respectful criticism of project decisions, policies or leadership is permitted. This policy must not be used to suppress good-faith disagreement or legitimate concerns.

3.4 Accountability

Participants must take reasonable responsibility for their conduct, correct avoidable harm where appropriate and cooperate honestly with legitimate reviews of reported misconduct.

Role, seniority, contribution history, popularity, financial involvement or administrative access does not exempt anyone from this policy.

4. Unacceptable Conduct

The following conduct is prohibited in covered project spaces.

4.1 Harassment and Intimidation

Examples include:

* Threatening, stalking, bullying or deliberately intimidating another participant.
* Repeated unwanted contact after a clear request to stop.
* Coordinated harassment, targeted humiliation or malicious personal attacks.
* Unwelcome sexual attention, sexual harassment or coercive behavior.
* Threats of violence or encouragement of violence against another person.
* Deliberately creating a hostile environment to discourage someone’s participation.

4.2 Discrimination and Hateful Conduct

Examples include:

* Slurs, demeaning stereotypes or hateful language directed at individuals or groups.
* Exclusionary conduct intended to disadvantage people based on protected characteristics.
* Degrading comments about a person’s identity, disability, background or socioeconomic circumstances.
* Targeted humiliation intended to drive a participant out of the project.
* Discriminatory denial of project opportunities or privileges where the decision falls within the project’s authority.

4.3 Disruptive or Abusive Participation

Examples include:

* Trolling, persistent bad-faith disruption or repeated derailing of project discussions.
* Deliberate interference with code review, project operations or legitimate moderation.
* Abusing administrative permissions, repository access or organizational authority.
* Repeatedly submitting hostile or threatening content after reasonable moderation instructions.
* Creating or using additional accounts to evade legitimate moderation restrictions.
* Deliberately disrupting meetings or collaboration activities to prevent others from participating.

Good-faith disagreement, responsible security research, respectful reporting of misconduct and legitimate criticism are not prohibited merely because they are uncomfortable or critical.

4.4 Privacy Violations and Doxxing

Participants must not publish, obtain, use or disclose another person’s private information without appropriate authorization or another valid legal basis.

Prohibited conduct includes:

* Publishing private contact details, home addresses, identity documents or confidential records to target, threaten or intimidate someone.
* Disclosing private messages or non-public material in violation of applicable confidentiality obligations, law or legitimate privacy rights.
* Exposing passwords, authentication tokens, private keys or confidential repository information.
* Accessing, collecting or disclosing personal information beyond authorized project responsibilities.
* Using personal information to facilitate harassment, impersonation or unauthorized access.

Security-sensitive and personal information discovered during project activities must be handled through appropriate authorized channels.

4.5 Retaliation and Abuse of Authority

Retaliation is prohibited against anyone who, in good faith:

* Reports a possible policy violation.
* Provides relevant information during a review.
* Requests assistance or reasonable protective measures.
* Participates honestly in an authorized investigation.
* Declines to engage in abusive or inappropriate conduct.

Retaliation may include threats, harassment, unjustified exclusion, misuse of permissions, adverse treatment or attempts to intimidate a reporter or witness.

A good-faith report that cannot be substantiated is not, by itself, misconduct. Knowingly fabricated allegations, deliberately falsified evidence and malicious misuse of reporting procedures may be addressed separately.

4.6 Misuse of Project Resources

Participants must not use MedOrbits repositories, accounts, infrastructure, communications channels or other project resources to facilitate harassment, discrimination, fraud, unauthorized access, privacy violations or other prohibited conduct.

5. Education, Admissions and Applicant Information

Because MedOrbits may handle education, admissions, recruitment and application-related information, participants must exercise particular care when working with potentially sensitive material.

Participants must:

1. Use synthetic or appropriately anonymized information in examples, tests and demonstrations wherever practical.
2. Avoid publishing real applicant records, identity documents, academic records, contact details or confidential application materials in public issues, pull requests or repositories.
3. Access personal information only when authorized and necessary for a legitimate project responsibility.
4. Report suspected privacy incidents, exposed records or unauthorized access through an appropriate, verified channel.
5. Avoid presenting unverified admissions, eligibility, recruitment or institutional information as authoritative fact.
6. Respect applicable confidentiality obligations and relevant privacy and data-protection requirements.
7. Avoid collecting or retaining personal information beyond what is authorized and necessary for the relevant task.

A Code of Conduct report should contain only the information reasonably necessary to explain the concern. Do not attach sensitive documents, passwords, credentials or private records unless an authorized and secure process specifically requires them.

This section establishes behavioral expectations. It does not replace MedOrbits’ privacy, security, data-retention or access-control policies.

6. Responsibilities of Maintainers and Project Leadership

People entrusted with project administration, review or moderation responsibilities must apply this policy fairly and consistently.

Maintainers and authorized reviewers are expected to:

* Communicate standards and moderation decisions clearly.
* Assess reported concerns impartially and in good faith.
* Consider relevant evidence, context, impact and applicable policy.
* Identify conflicts of interest and arrange an appropriate alternative reviewer where feasible.
* Protect sensitive information on a need-to-know basis.
* Take reasonable steps to prevent retaliation.
* Explain decisions when appropriate without unnecessarily disclosing confidential information.
* Apply corrective measures proportionately and consistently.
* Review their own conduct against the same standards applied to other participants.
* Exercise administrative privileges only for legitimate project purposes.

Administrative access is a responsibility, not a privilege to silence criticism or avoid accountability.

Where a complaint concerns a maintainer or the person normally responsible for enforcement, that person must not be the sole decision-maker for the complaint. An independent authorized reviewer should be sought where reasonably available.

If an independent reviewer is unavailable, the project should disclose that limitation to the extent appropriate and consider an external or organizational review route where feasible.

No individual should be represented as an independent reviewer unless that role has actually been established.

7. Reporting a Concern

Anyone who experiences or witnesses conduct that may violate this policy is encouraged to report it.

7.1 Reporting Channel

Reports should be submitted through a reporting channel officially designated and verified by MedOrbits for Code of Conduct concerns.

Reporting contact: To be confirmed before publication.

This document does not establish an email address, telephone number, response deadline, confidential reporting service or dedicated moderation team.

Before this policy is presented as operational, the project should designate a responsible recipient, publish a reliable reporting method and establish a process for handling reports involving that recipient.

7.2 Information to Include

Where safe and appropriate, a report may include:

* A description of the conduct or incident.
* The approximate date, time and relevant project location.
* The usernames or roles of the people involved, where known.
* Relevant links, messages or other supporting evidence.
* The impact of the conduct and any immediate safety concerns.
* Any reasonable request concerning contact, privacy or protection from retaliation.

Reporters are not expected to conduct their own investigations or obtain evidence they cannot safely access.

Reports should distinguish, where possible, between directly observed facts, information received from others and personal interpretations.

7.3 Immediate Safety and Security Concerns

If there is an immediate threat to physical safety, contact the appropriate local emergency services or competent authority.

For exposed credentials, private keys or suspected security vulnerabilities, use the designated security-reporting process in SECURITY.md after verifying that the process is operational. Avoid publishing exploitable details or sensitive information publicly.

A Code of Conduct reporting channel must not be assumed to be a security incident response service.

8. Review and Investigation Process

Reports should be handled through a fair, proportionate and privacy-conscious process.

Once an appropriate reporting and review process has been established, the responsible reviewer should generally:

1. Acknowledge the report, where contact details and circumstances permit.
2. Assess immediate risks, including threats, continuing harassment, retaliation or exposure of personal information.
3. Confirm scope and responsibility by assessing whether the reported conduct falls under this policy and identifying potential conflicts of interest.
4. Preserve relevant evidence where necessary and lawful, taking reasonable precautions against unauthorized access or alteration.
5. Review the available information fairly, including relevant context and responses from involved parties where appropriate.
6. Determine an appropriate response based on the available evidence, severity, impact, recurrence, applicable policy and the project’s authority.
7. Communicate the outcome appropriately, subject to privacy, confidentiality, safety and other legitimate restrictions.
8. Consider follow-up measures where needed to reduce recurrence, prevent retaliation or protect affected participants.

The process may be adapted to the seriousness and urgency of the incident. Reasonable interim protective measures may be appropriate before a review is complete.

No outcome should be predetermined solely by a person’s role, reputation, seniority or relationship with the project.

The project must not promise a specific investigation timeline unless it has established the resources and procedures needed to meet it.

9. Confidentiality and Privacy of Reports

Reports should be shared only with individuals who reasonably need the information to assess, investigate or address the concern, subject to applicable law and legitimate operational requirements.

The project should take reasonable steps to:

* Limit unnecessary disclosure of a reporter’s identity.
* Protect relevant evidence against unauthorized access or alteration.
* Avoid publicly disclosing private details about the people involved.
* Minimize the collection and retention of personal information.
* Communicate relevant confidentiality limitations where appropriate.
* Handle records according to applicable privacy and retention requirements.

Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed. Information may need to be disclosed to investigate a concern, protect individuals, comply with law or provide a fair opportunity to respond.

Reporters and witnesses should avoid public speculation, harassment or unnecessary disclosure of information relating to an ongoing review.

Confidentiality must not be used to conceal wrongdoing, prevent lawful reporting or suppress legitimate criticism.

10. Enforcement and Corrective Action

Where a violation is established, or an interim protective response is reasonably necessary, an authorized project decision-maker may take action proportionate to the circumstances and within the project’s authority.

Possible measures include the following.

Level 1 — Guidance or Correction

For minor or first-time conduct where appropriate, the participant may receive an explanation of the relevant standard and a request to correct the behavior.

Level 2 — Formal Warning

A written warning may describe the conduct, explain the concern and specify the expected changes in behavior.

Level 3 — Content or Access Restrictions

Where justified, the project may remove or restrict inappropriate content, limit participation in a discussion, temporarily suspend permissions or establish reasonable participation boundaries.

Level 4 — Temporary Suspension

For serious, repeated or continuing misconduct, the project may temporarily restrict access to covered project spaces or administrative privileges.

Level 5 — Removal from Project Participation

For severe misconduct, repeated violations, retaliation, credible threats or other circumstances warranting stronger action, the project may remove a participant from project spaces, revoke project access or discontinue a collaboration, subject to applicable agreements and law.

These levels are illustrative, not mandatory steps. Serious incidents may warrant immediate action without prior warnings.

When deciding on a response, the project should consider:

* The nature and severity of the conduct.
* Its impact on affected individuals and the wider project.
* Whether the conduct was repeated or continued after a warning.
* The available evidence and relevant context.
* Any immediate safety, privacy or retaliation risks.
* Relevant mitigating or aggravating circumstances.
* The proportionality and consistency of the proposed measure.

Nothing in this policy authorizes unlawful discrimination, arbitrary action contrary to binding agreements or penalties beyond the project’s lawful authority.

11. Appeals and Review of Decisions

Where a suitable review process exists, a person affected by a significant enforcement decision may request reconsideration by an authorized reviewer who was not the sole decision-maker in the original matter.

An appeal may identify:

* Material factual information that was overlooked.
* A relevant conflict of interest.
* A significant misunderstanding of the applicable policy.
* New evidence that could reasonably affect the decision.
* A sanction that appears disproportionate to the circumstances.

Appeals should be assessed impartially where a suitable reviewer is available. Interim measures may remain in place where reasonably necessary to address credible safety, privacy or retaliation risks.

Repeated appeals that introduce no material new information may be declined, subject to applicable law and any binding agreement.

The project must not claim to offer an independent appeal mechanism until the relevant reviewer and process have been established.

12. Good-Faith Reporting and Due Process

Reports must be assessed according to the available evidence and circumstances, not dismissed merely because they are uncomfortable, critical or initially difficult to verify.

A report made in good faith is not a policy violation simply because the review cannot substantiate it.

Knowingly fabricated allegations, deliberately falsified evidence, malicious impersonation or intentional misuse of the reporting process may themselves constitute misconduct.

A fair review should distinguish between:

* An honest report that cannot be substantiated.
* A report containing an unintentional mistake.
* An allegation that is intentionally deceptive or malicious.

No person should be punished merely for making a good-faith report, requesting clarification or participating honestly in a review.

13. External Platforms and Related Policies

MedOrbits may use third-party repositories, hosting services, communication tools and other external platforms.

Participants must comply with applicable platform rules and law in addition to this policy. Platform administrators may exercise their own moderation powers independently.

This Code of Conduct does not claim authority over systems, organizations or individuals outside the project’s legitimate scope of control.

Where conduct may also involve a security incident, privacy breach, contractual issue or legal violation, the matter may require a separate process under the relevant policy or law.

Related project documents may include:

* SECURITY.md
* SUPPORT_GUIDE.md
* LICENSE
* README.md
* Applicable privacy notices and service terms

The existence of a referenced document does not establish that an unverified reporting channel or operational procedure is active.

14. Communication, Training and Policy Review

MedOrbits should make this Code of Conduct reasonably accessible to contributors and participants in covered project spaces.

Recommended implementation measures include:

* Linking this policy from README.md and contribution guidance.
* Informing contributors how to report concerns.
* Designating a responsible enforcement contact or team.
* Establishing a procedure for conflicts of interest.
* Defining an appropriate review and appeal process.
* Reviewing moderation procedures periodically.
* Updating related documentation when reporting or enforcement arrangements change.

This policy should be reviewed when project governance, reporting arrangements, moderation responsibilities or applicable requirements materially change.

Updates should be communicated appropriately to affected participants. Changes should not be represented as retroactively altering binding agreements unless legally effective.

15. Acknowledgment and Participation

Participation in MedOrbits project spaces is expected to be consistent with this Code of Conduct and other applicable terms governing that participation.

Contributors and collaborators are encouraged to read this policy, seek clarification where needed and help maintain a respectful, responsible and constructive working environment.

No participant is required to tolerate harassment, conceal misconduct or abandon a good-faith concern to remain involved in the project.

16. Interpretation and Limitations

This Code of Conduct establishes behavioral expectations for covered MedOrbits project spaces. It does not guarantee that misconduct will never occur, that every report can be independently verified or that every dispute can be resolved to every participant’s satisfaction.

Its interpretation and application remain subject to applicable law, binding agreements and the actual authority of the individuals administering the project.

If any provision is found unenforceable, the remaining provisions should continue to apply to the extent permitted by law.

This policy does not replace legal advice, emergency services, formal workplace procedures, statutory reporting obligations or other protections available under applicable law.

Final Policy Statement

MedOrbits is committed to respectful collaboration, responsible conduct, privacy, fairness and accountability.

Every participant shares responsibility for maintaining an environment in which people can contribute ideas, improve software, discuss problems and work toward common goals without harassment, discrimination, intimidation or retaliation.

Concerns should be handled seriously, impartially and with appropriate care for everyone involved.

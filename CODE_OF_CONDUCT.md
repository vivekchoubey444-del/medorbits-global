1. Purpose and Commitment

MedOrbits is committed to maintaining a professional, respectful, inclusive and responsible environment for people who contribute to, maintain, review, use or represent the project.

MedOrbits covers education, admissions, career guidance, recruitment, application services, software development and informational resources. These activities require responsible communication, respect for individual dignity, careful handling of information and appropriate accountability.

This Code of Conduct establishes the behavioral standards expected in MedOrbits project spaces and in interactions where individuals officially represent the project.

MedOrbits recognizes that constructive disagreement, rigorous technical review, honest criticism and diverse perspectives can improve project outcomes when expressed responsibly.

Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, retaliation, abuse of authority and unauthorized disclosure of sensitive information are incompatible with these standards.

Every participant shares responsibility for maintaining a working environment in which people can participate without being subjected to abusive or threatening behavior.

1.1 Policy objectives

This policy aims to:

* Establish clear standards for professional conduct.
* Protect participants from harassment, discrimination and retaliation.
* Encourage constructive technical discussion and responsible collaboration.
* Protect personal information and confidential project material.
* Provide a defined framework for reporting and reviewing concerns.
* Support fair, proportionate and consistent enforcement.
* Establish accountability for people exercising project authority.
* Encourage appropriate correction, prevention and learning after incidents.

1.2 Guiding principles

Principle	Commitment
Respect	Treat people with dignity, including during disagreement
Safety	Take reasonable action to address credible threats and harmful conduct
Fairness	Assess concerns using relevant evidence, context and consistent standards
Privacy	Minimize unnecessary collection, disclosure and retention of sensitive information
Accountability	Apply the same behavioral expectations to contributors and leadership
Impartiality	Address conflicts of interest and avoid predetermined outcomes
Proportionality	Match corrective action to the seriousness and circumstances of a violation
Transparency	Explain policies and decisions appropriately without exposing confidential information
Non-retaliation	Protect good-faith reporting and participation in legitimate reviews

2. Scope and Applicability

This policy applies to participation in MedOrbits project activities and interactions, including:

* Source-code repositories, commits, pull requests and code reviews.
* Issue trackers, technical discussions and project documentation.
* Project-related chat rooms, mailing lists and collaboration platforms.
* Meetings, workshops, planning sessions and events organized by MedOrbits.
* Online events and in-person activities organized or officially represented by the project.
* Communications through official project accounts.
* Conduct undertaken while representing MedOrbits in a professional capacity.
* Interactions involving maintainers, contributors, reviewers, contractors, volunteers and other authorized participants.

2.1 Off-platform conduct

Off-platform conduct may fall within this policy when there is a clear and relevant connection to project participation, official representation, participant safety, project access or the integrity of project activities.

This policy is not intended to regulate unrelated private conduct merely because an individual participates in MedOrbits.

Any response to off-platform conduct must remain within the project’s legitimate authority and respect applicable law, contractual obligations and relevant privacy rights.

2.2 Relationship to other policies

This policy operates alongside, and does not replace:

* Applicable laws and legal obligations.
* Repository and platform terms.
* CONTRIBUTING.md.
* SECURITY.md.
* Applicable privacy notices and data-handling policies.
* License terms and contractual obligations.
* Employment, contractor or organizational policies where applicable.
* Applicable emergency, safeguarding or incident-response procedures.

Where different requirements apply, the project must identify the relevant obligation and obtain appropriate advice when necessary. This document does not independently determine legal precedence among every possible policy or agreement.

3. Expected Community Standards

All participants must act in a manner consistent with the following standards.

3.1 Respect and professionalism

Participants must:

* Communicate courteously and professionally.
* Critique ideas, implementations and decisions rather than attacking individuals.
* Avoid insults, humiliation, intimidation and deliberately hostile communication.
* Respect reasonable personal boundaries and requests to discontinue unwanted contact.
* Recognize the time, expertise and effort contributed by others.
* Use language appropriate to the context and audience.
* Address disagreements through constructive discussion and established review processes.
* Avoid using status, access privileges or technical authority to demean others.

3.2 Inclusion and equal treatment

Harassment or discriminatory treatment based on characteristics protected by applicable law is prohibited.

Examples of protected or sensitive characteristics may include race, caste, ethnicity, nationality, religion, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, age and socioeconomic background, subject to applicable law.

Participants must not use differences in education, experience, language, technical ability, communication style or professional seniority as a justification for mistreatment.

Project opportunities, review decisions and access privileges must not be used to discriminate unlawfully or retaliate against a participant.

3.3 Constructive collaboration

Participants are expected to:

* Provide clear, relevant and actionable feedback.
* Support technical concerns with evidence or reproducible examples where practical.
* Accept reasonable criticism without personal retaliation.
* Acknowledge mistakes and correct them responsibly.
* Follow applicable review and contribution procedures.
* Respect reasonable moderation instructions.
* Escalate unresolved disagreements through appropriate channels.
* Avoid deliberate obstruction of legitimate project work.

Good-faith disagreement, responsible security research, criticism of project leadership and reporting of misconduct are permitted. This policy must not be used to suppress legitimate concerns or protect decision-makers from accountability.

3.4 Accountability

Every participant is accountable for their conduct.

Role, seniority, contribution history, popularity, financial involvement, employment status or administrative access does not exempt anyone from this policy.

Participants must not knowingly falsify evidence, misuse reporting procedures or interfere with a legitimate review.

Where appropriate, participants should cooperate with reasonable corrective measures intended to prevent further harm.

4. Prohibited Conduct

The following conduct is prohibited in covered project spaces.

4.1 Harassment and intimidation

Examples include:

* Threatening, stalking, bullying or deliberately intimidating another participant.
* Repeated unwanted contact after a clear request to stop.
* Coordinated harassment or targeted humiliation.
* Unwelcome sexual attention, sexual harassment or coercive behavior.
* Threats of violence or encouragement of violence against another person.
* Deliberately creating a hostile environment to discourage participation.
* Using repeated abusive messages to pressure another participant into abandoning a legitimate concern.

4.2 Discrimination and hateful conduct

Examples include:

* Slurs, hateful language or demeaning stereotypes directed at individuals or groups.
* Discriminatory exclusion based on protected characteristics.
* Degrading comments about identity, disability, background or socioeconomic circumstances.
* Targeted humiliation intended to drive a participant out of the project.
* Unlawful denial of project opportunities or privileges.

4.3 Abusive or disruptive participation

Examples include:

* Persistent bad-faith disruption of technical discussions.
* Deliberately derailing meetings or review sessions to prevent participation.
* Interfering with legitimate moderation or project operations.
* Abusing administrative permissions or organizational authority.
* Evading legitimate access restrictions through additional accounts.
* Repeatedly submitting threatening or abusive content after reasonable moderation instructions.
* Deliberately obstructing legitimate code review or incident response.

Firm technical disagreement, good-faith criticism and reasonable challenges to project decisions are not prohibited merely because they are uncomfortable.

4.4 Privacy violations and doxxing

Participants must not obtain, use, publish or disclose another person’s private information without appropriate authorization or another valid legal basis.

Prohibited conduct includes:

* Publishing private contact details or home addresses to target, threaten or intimidate someone.
* Disclosing identity documents or confidential records without authorization.
* Exposing passwords, authentication tokens, private keys or confidential credentials.
* Accessing personal information beyond authorized project responsibilities.
* Using personal information to facilitate harassment, impersonation or unauthorized access.
* Sharing confidential messages or private records in violation of applicable law, legitimate privacy rights or binding confidentiality obligations.

Information discovered during project activities must be handled through appropriate authorized channels.

4.5 Retaliation

Retaliation is prohibited against anyone who, in good faith:

* Reports a possible violation.
* Provides relevant information during a review.
* Requests assistance or reasonable protective measures.
* Participates honestly in an authorized investigation.
* Declines to engage in abusive or inappropriate conduct.
* Raises a legitimate concern about project safety, privacy or governance.

Retaliation may include threats, harassment, unjustified exclusion, misuse of permissions, adverse treatment or attempts to intimidate a reporter or witness.

A good-faith report that cannot be substantiated is not, by itself, misconduct. Knowingly fabricated allegations and deliberate falsification of evidence may be addressed separately.

4.6 Misuse of project resources

MedOrbits repositories, accounts, infrastructure, communication channels and other project resources must not be used to facilitate harassment, discrimination, fraud, unauthorized access, privacy violations or other prohibited conduct.

Participants must not use project access to obtain information for personal retaliation, unauthorized surveillance or unrelated purposes.

5. Education, Admissions and Applicant Information

Because MedOrbits may handle education, admissions, recruitment and application-related information, participants must exercise particular care when working with potentially sensitive material.

5.1 Data-handling requirements

Participants must:

1. Use synthetic or appropriately anonymized information in examples, tests and demonstrations wherever practical.
2. Avoid publishing real applicant records, identity documents, academic records, contact details or confidential application materials in public issues, pull requests or repositories.
3. Access personal information only when authorized and necessary for a legitimate project responsibility.
4. Avoid collecting, copying or retaining personal information beyond what is authorized and necessary.
5. Protect private documents against unauthorized access or disclosure.
6. Report suspected privacy incidents, exposed records or unauthorized access through an appropriate verified channel.
7. Respect applicable confidentiality obligations and relevant data-protection requirements.
8. Avoid presenting unverified admissions, eligibility, recruitment or institutional information as authoritative fact.

5.2 Sensitive information in reports

A Code of Conduct report should contain only the information reasonably necessary to explain the concern.

Do not attach passwords, authentication tokens, private keys, unnecessary identity documents or unrelated personal records.

If sensitive evidence is necessary, use an authorized and appropriately protected reporting process. Do not assume that a public issue tracker, ordinary email account or general-purpose chat channel is confidential.

5.3 Separation of responsibilities

This section establishes behavioral expectations. It does not replace the project’s security, privacy, access-control, retention or incident-response policies.

Security vulnerabilities must be handled through the appropriate security-reporting process. Suspected privacy incidents may require a separate response under applicable policy or law.

6. Responsibilities of Maintainers and Project Leadership

Individuals entrusted with project administration, moderation, review or enforcement responsibilities must apply this policy fairly and consistently.

They are expected to:

* Communicate behavioral standards and moderation decisions clearly.
* Assess reported concerns impartially and in good faith.
* Consider relevant evidence, context, impact and applicable policy.
* Identify conflicts of interest and arrange an alternative reviewer where feasible.
* Protect sensitive information on a need-to-know basis.
* Take reasonable steps to prevent retaliation.
* Explain decisions appropriately without unnecessarily disclosing confidential information.
* Apply corrective measures proportionately.
* Exercise administrative privileges only for legitimate project purposes.
* Follow the same behavioral standards imposed on other participants.
* Avoid using moderation powers to suppress legitimate criticism.
* Document significant enforcement decisions securely and appropriately.

Administrative access is a responsibility, not an entitlement to silence criticism or avoid accountability.

6.1 Conflicts of interest

A person must not be the sole decision-maker for a complaint where that person is the subject of the complaint or has a material conflict that could reasonably compromise impartiality.

Where feasible, the complaint should be assigned to another authorized reviewer who is not materially involved in the incident.

If no suitable independent reviewer is available, the project should disclose the limitation where appropriate and consider an alternative review route.

The project must not claim to offer independent oversight unless that arrangement has actually been established.

7. Reporting a Concern

Anyone who experiences or witnesses conduct that may violate this policy is encouraged to report it.

7.1 Official reporting channel

Reports must be submitted through a reporting channel officially designated and verified by MedOrbits for Code of Conduct concerns.

Reporting contact: To be designated before operational publication.

This document does not establish a working email address, telephone number, confidential reporting service, response deadline or dedicated moderation team.

Before representing this policy as operational, authorized project leadership must designate a responsible recipient, publish a reliable reporting method and establish a procedure for reports involving that recipient.

7.2 Information to include

Where safe and appropriate, a report may include:

* A description of the conduct or incident.
* The approximate date and time.
* The relevant project space, page or communication channel.
* Usernames or roles of the people involved, where known.
* Relevant links or supporting evidence.
* The impact of the conduct.
* Any immediate safety, privacy or retaliation concerns.
* Reasonable requests concerning contact, privacy or protective measures.

Reporters are not expected to investigate incidents themselves or obtain evidence they cannot safely access.

Where possible, distinguish direct observations from information received from others and personal interpretations.

7.3 Anonymous reports

Anonymous reports may be considered if the established reporting channel supports them.

The ability to assess an anonymous report may be limited when follow-up questions or corroborating evidence are unavailable. Lack of identifying information should not automatically result in dismissal where credible information supports further assessment.

Do not advertise anonymous reporting as an available service until a suitable mechanism has been implemented.

7.4 Immediate safety concerns

If there is an immediate threat to physical safety, contact appropriate local emergency services or a competent authority.

For exposed credentials, private keys or suspected security vulnerabilities, use the designated security-reporting process described in SECURITY.md after verifying that the process is operational.

Avoid publishing exploitable details or sensitive information publicly.

A Code of Conduct reporting channel must not be assumed to be an emergency service or a security incident response service.

8. Review and Investigation Process

Reports should be handled through a fair, proportionate and privacy-conscious process.

Once a suitable reporting and review process has been established, the responsible reviewer should generally:

1. Acknowledge the report where contact details and circumstances permit.
2. Assess immediate risks, including credible threats, continuing harassment, retaliation or exposure of personal information.
3. Confirm scope and responsibility by assessing whether the reported conduct falls under this policy and identifying conflicts of interest.
4. Preserve relevant evidence where necessary and lawful, taking reasonable precautions against unauthorized access or alteration.
5. Review available information fairly, including relevant context and responses from involved parties where appropriate.
6. Determine an appropriate response based on the evidence, severity, impact, recurrence, applicable policy and the project’s authority.
7. Communicate the outcome appropriately, subject to privacy, confidentiality, safety and other legitimate restrictions.
8. Consider follow-up measures where needed to reduce recurrence, prevent retaliation or protect affected participants.

8.1 Interim protective measures

Reasonable interim measures may be appropriate when necessary to protect participants, preserve evidence, restrict continuing harassment or prevent unauthorized access.

Examples may include temporary restrictions on communication, moderation of harmful content or suspension of access to an affected project resource.

Interim measures are precautionary and must not automatically be presented as a final finding of misconduct.

8.2 Fairness and proportionality

No outcome should be predetermined solely by a person’s role, reputation, seniority or relationship with the project.

The reviewer should consider the available evidence, relevant context, potential impact and applicable policy before reaching a conclusion.

The process may be adapted to the seriousness and urgency of the incident.

The project must not promise a particular investigation timeline unless the resources and procedures needed to meet that timeline have been established.

9. Confidentiality and Privacy of Reports

Reports should be shared only with individuals who reasonably need the information to assess, investigate or address the concern, subject to applicable law and legitimate operational requirements.

The project should take reasonable steps to:

* Limit unnecessary disclosure of a reporter’s identity.
* Protect relevant evidence against unauthorized access or alteration.
* Avoid publicly disclosing private details about the people involved.
* Minimize the collection and retention of personal information.
* Restrict access to report records to authorized individuals.
* Communicate relevant confidentiality limitations where appropriate.
* Handle records according to applicable privacy and retention requirements.

9.1 Limits of confidentiality

Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed.

Information may need to be disclosed to investigate a concern, protect individuals, comply with law or provide a fair opportunity to respond.

Reporters and witnesses should avoid public speculation, harassment or unnecessary disclosure of information relating to an ongoing review.

Confidentiality must not be used to conceal wrongdoing, prevent lawful reporting or suppress legitimate criticism.

9.2 Evidence handling

Where records are retained, the responsible project authority should establish reasonable safeguards appropriate to their sensitivity.

These may include access restrictions, secure storage, documented access where appropriate and deletion in accordance with the applicable retention policy.

Do not collect extensive private information merely because it may be convenient for an investigation.

Do not claim that records are encrypted, access-logged, independently audited or securely deleted unless the relevant controls have been implemented and verified.

10. Enforcement and Corrective Action

Where a violation is established, or an interim protective response is reasonably necessary, an authorized decision-maker may take action proportionate to the circumstances and within the project’s authority.

10.1 Possible responses

Level	Measure	Appropriate circumstances
1	Guidance or correction	Minor conduct where a clear explanation and correction may be sufficient
2	Formal warning	Conduct requiring a documented warning and clear behavioral expectations
3	Content or access restrictions	Harmful content, repeated disruption or a need to establish participation boundaries
4	Temporary suspension	Serious, repeated or continuing misconduct
5	Removal from project participation	Severe misconduct, credible threats, retaliation, repeated violations or other circumstances warranting stronger action

These levels are illustrative, not mandatory steps. Serious incidents may warrant immediate action without prior warnings.

10.2 Factors in determining a response

Where appropriate, the decision-maker should consider:

* Nature and severity of the conduct.
* Impact on affected individuals and the wider project.
* Whether the conduct was repeated or continued after a warning.
* Available evidence and relevant context.
* Immediate safety, privacy or retaliation risks.
* Relevant mitigating or aggravating circumstances.
* Proportionality and consistency with comparable cases.
* The project’s authority to impose the proposed measure.

10.3 Scope of enforcement

Possible actions may include content moderation, communication restrictions, temporary access suspension, revocation of project permissions or discontinuation of a collaboration.

Any action must remain within the project’s legitimate authority and applicable agreements and law.

Nothing in this policy authorizes unlawful discrimination, arbitrary action contrary to binding agreements or penalties beyond the project’s lawful authority.

10.4 Documentation of decisions

Significant enforcement decisions should be documented sufficiently to support accountability, consistency and appropriate review.

Such records should contain only information reasonably necessary for those purposes and must be protected against unauthorized access.

Do not publish private disciplinary records or confidential investigative material merely to demonstrate that an enforcement action occurred.

11. Appeals and Review of Decisions

Where a suitable review process exists, a person affected by a significant enforcement decision may request reconsideration by an authorized reviewer who was not the sole decision-maker in the original matter.

An appeal may identify:

* Material factual information that was overlooked.
* A relevant conflict of interest.
* A significant misunderstanding of the applicable policy.
* New evidence that could reasonably affect the decision.
* A response that appears disproportionate to the circumstances.

Appeals should be assessed impartially where a suitable reviewer is available.

Interim measures may remain in place where reasonably necessary to address credible safety, privacy or retaliation risks.

Repeated appeals that introduce no material new information may be declined, subject to applicable law and binding agreements.

The project must not claim to offer an independent appeal mechanism until the relevant reviewer and procedure have been established.

12. Good-Faith Reporting and Due Process

Reports must be assessed according to available evidence and circumstances.

A report must not be dismissed merely because it is uncomfortable, critical or initially difficult to verify.

A good-faith report is not a policy violation solely because the review cannot substantiate it.

The project should distinguish between:

* An honest report that cannot be substantiated.
* A report containing an unintentional mistake.
* An allegation that is intentionally deceptive or malicious.
* Deliberately fabricated evidence or impersonation.

Knowingly fabricated allegations, deliberate falsification of evidence and malicious misuse of reporting procedures may themselves constitute misconduct.

No participant should be punished merely for making a good-faith report, requesting clarification or participating honestly in a review.

14. Communication, Training and Policy Review

MedOrbits should make this Code of Conduct reasonably accessible to contributors and participants in covered project spaces.

Recommended implementation measures include:

* Linking the policy from README.md and CONTRIBUTING.md.
* Informing contributors how to report concerns.
* Designating a responsible enforcement contact or team.
* Establishing a procedure for conflicts of interest.
* Defining an appropriate review and appeal process.
* Limiting access to sensitive reports.
* Reviewing moderation and incident-handling procedures periodically.
* Updating related documentation when reporting or enforcement arrangements change.

The policy should be reviewed when project governance, reporting arrangements, moderation responsibilities or applicable requirements materially change.

Updates should be communicated appropriately to affected participants. Changes must not be represented as retroactively altering binding agreements unless legally effective.

15. Acknowledgment and Participation

Participation in MedOrbits project spaces is expected to be consistent with this Code of Conduct and other applicable terms governing that participation.

Contributors and collaborators are encouraged to read this policy, seek clarification where needed and help maintain a respectful, responsible and constructive working environment.

No participant is required to tolerate harassment, conceal misconduct or abandon a good-faith concern to remain involved in the project.

The project should communicate material policy changes and any relevant changes to reporting or enforcement arrangements.

16. Interpretation and Limitations

This Code of Conduct establishes behavioral expectations for covered MedOrbits project spaces.

It does not guarantee that misconduct will never occur, that every report can be independently verified or that every dispute can be resolved to every participant’s satisfaction.

Its interpretation and application remain subject to applicable law, binding agreements and the actual authority of the individuals administering the project.

If any provision is found unenforceable, the remaining provisions should continue to apply to the extent permitted by law.

This policy does not replace legal advice, emergency services, formal workplace procedures, statutory reporting obligations or other protections available under applicable law.

18. Final Policy Statement

MedOrbits is committed to respectful collaboration, responsible conduct, privacy, fairness and accountability.

Every participant shares responsibility for maintaining an environment in which people can contribute ideas, improve software, discuss problems and work toward common goals without harassment, discrimination, intimidation or retaliation.

Concerns should be handled seriously, impartially and with appropriate care for everyone involved.

Project leadership is responsible for ensuring that the policy is supported by workable reporting, review and enforcement arrangements rather than relying on written commitments alone.

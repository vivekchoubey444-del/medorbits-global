MedOrbits Code of Conduct

Policy version: 1.0
Last reviewed: October 16, 2026
Applies to: All MedOrbits project participants and covered community spaces

1. Our Commitment

MedOrbits is committed to maintaining a safe, respectful, inclusive, and constructive environment for everyone participating in its project and community spaces.

We welcome contributions from people of all experience levels, professional backgrounds, identities, and perspectives. Whether contributing code, reporting a bug, proposing a feature, reviewing a pull request, participating in a discussion, or improving documentation, every participant has a role in maintaining the quality and integrity of the project.

All participants are expected to communicate professionally, respect personal boundaries, protect privacy, and collaborate in good faith.

This Code of Conduct establishes the behavioral standards, reporting procedures, and enforcement principles applicable to participation in the MedOrbits project.

2. Scope

This policy applies to all participants in MedOrbits project spaces and to conduct connected with representing or participating in the project, including:

* GitHub repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, and code reviews.
* Technical conversations, documentation contributions, and collaboration channels.
* Online meetings, community events, and project-related communications.
* Activities performed while officially representing MedOrbits.
* Conduct outside official project spaces when it materially affects participant safety or the integrity of the project community.

These standards apply equally to maintainers, administrators, contributors, reviewers, collaborators, and community members, regardless of seniority, role, or access privileges.

3. Expected Behavior

All participants are expected to:

* Communicate respectfully: Be courteous, patient, professional, and constructive.
* Critique ideas, not individuals: Focus feedback on code, proposals, decisions, and observable behavior rather than personal attacks.
* Collaborate in good faith: Explain disagreements clearly, consider alternative approaches, and seek practical solutions.
* Use inclusive language: Avoid discriminatory, degrading, or exclusionary language.
* Respect boundaries: Accept that others may decline conversations, requests, or interactions.
* Protect privacy: Do not disclose personal information, confidential records, private communications, or credentials without authorization.
* Provide constructive feedback: Identify problems clearly and, where practical, suggest improvements.
* Follow project processes: Respect contribution guidelines, review procedures, security-reporting requirements, and reasonable moderation decisions.
* Acknowledge contributions: Give appropriate credit and do not misrepresent another person’s work.
* Act responsibly: Report genuine concerns through appropriate channels and avoid presenting unverified allegations as established facts.

Technical mistakes, respectful criticism, and good-faith differences of opinion are not automatically violations of this policy. They should be addressed through constructive discussion and appropriate project procedures.

4. Unacceptable Behavior

The following conduct is prohibited within the scope of this policy.

4.1 Harassment and discrimination

* Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, stalking, or threats.
* Unwelcome sexual attention, sexual harassment, or directed sexualized conduct.
* Abusive or degrading comments relating to personal characteristics or identity.
* Deliberate humiliation, personal insults, or repeated hostile targeting.

4.2 Disruptive or bad-faith conduct

* Trolling or sustained disruption of constructive discussions.
* Repeatedly disregarding reasonable personal boundaries or moderation instructions.
* Deliberately misrepresenting another participant’s statements or contributions to cause harm.
* Obstructing collaboration or reviews through abusive or deliberately bad-faith conduct.

4.3 Privacy and security violations

* Publishing private information, confidential communications, or personal records without authorization.
* Sharing passwords, access tokens, private keys, session cookies, or other authentication secrets.
* Attempting unauthorized access to accounts, repositories, systems, or infrastructure.
* Exposing private user data or exploiting vulnerabilities in ways that unnecessarily endanger people or systems.
* Using project access or privileges to access, modify, or disclose information without authorization.

4.4 Retaliation and abuse of authority

* Retaliating against anyone who makes a good-faith report, participates in a review, or provides relevant information.
* Abusing maintainer, administrative, review, or moderation privileges to intimidate, silence, or unfairly disadvantage others.
* Making knowingly false or malicious reports intended to harass or punish another participant.

Respectful disagreement, good-faith reporting, legitimate criticism of project decisions, and authorized security research conducted within applicable rules are not prohibited merely because they are uncomfortable or critical.

5. Reporting a Concern

Anyone who experiences or witnesses conduct that may violate this policy is encouraged to report it privately to the MedOrbits project maintainers.

Use a reporting channel explicitly published in the root README.md or on the official MedOrbits website.

Where available and safe to share, include:

* A concise description of the incident.
* Relevant dates, times, repository links, issue references, or other context.
* The individuals or project spaces involved, where known.
* Supporting evidence that can be shared safely.
* Whether there is an immediate safety, privacy, or security concern.
* Any action you believe may help resolve the situation.

You do not need to provide every detail or confront the person involved before making a report.

Protect sensitive information

Do not include passwords, access tokens, private keys, identity documents, unrelated personal information, or unnecessary copies of confidential records.

If no private reporting contact is published, ask a maintainer through an appropriate channel to establish a private reporting method before sharing sensitive details. Do not submit sensitive allegations through public issues or pull requests.

For suspected security vulnerabilities, follow SECURITY.md and its dedicated reporting procedure.

For immediate threats or danger, contact appropriate local emergency or safety authorities where necessary. Project moderation is not a substitute for emergency assistance.

6. Report Handling and Confidentiality

Maintainers should handle reports impartially, discreetly, and in a manner proportionate to the circumstances and available resources.

Where practical, the handling process should include:

1. Acknowledgment: Confirm receipt when a safe response channel is available.
2. Initial assessment: Evaluate the reported conduct, potential risks, and any immediate protective measures.
3. Information gathering: Review relevant evidence and request clarification where necessary.
4. Fair consideration: Consider the available information, context, and responses from relevant participants where appropriate.
5. Decision: Determine whether the policy was violated and identify an appropriate response.
6. Communication: Inform relevant parties of the outcome to the extent appropriate and safe.
7. Follow-up: Consider additional measures to prevent recurrence or retaliation.

Reports will be handled confidentially to the extent reasonably practical. Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed, because reviewing a report may require sharing limited information with relevant individuals or responding to applicable legal obligations.

Access to reports and supporting evidence should be limited to people who need the information to assess or address the concern.

Maintainers should avoid unnecessary disclosure of a reporter’s identity, private communications, or sensitive evidence.

7. Enforcement and Possible Consequences

Maintainers are responsible for applying this policy fairly and consistently.

Depending on the severity, frequency, impact, context, and available evidence, responses may include:

1. Informal guidance: A private request to clarify expectations or correct minor conduct.
2. Formal warning: A clear notice that the behavior violates this policy and must stop.
3. Content moderation: Removal, editing, locking, or restriction of harmful content or discussions where permitted.
4. Temporary restrictions: Limits on commenting, reviewing, contributing, or participating in project spaces.
5. Suspension: Temporary suspension of participation or project privileges.
6. Permanent exclusion: Removal of project access or a permanent ban from covered community spaces where permitted.

Responses should be proportionate to the circumstances. Serious threats, harassment, unauthorized disclosure, or abuse of privileged access may warrant immediate protective action without prior informal warnings.

Where practical, maintainers should document significant enforcement decisions and explain the relevant expectations to affected participants.

Private information and details that could expose a reporter or another participant to harm must not be disclosed unnecessarily.

8. Appeals and Review

A participant who believes an enforcement decision was made in error may request a review through a private channel.

An appeal should identify:

* The decision being challenged.
* The reason for the challenge.
* Relevant additional information or evidence.

Where practical, an appeal should be reviewed by a maintainer or authorized reviewer who was not primarily responsible for the original decision.

If an independent reviewer is unavailable, the project should communicate that limitation.

An appeal does not automatically suspend an enforcement action. Interim restrictions may remain in place while a review is pending when necessary to protect participants or project systems.

Attempts to intimidate reporters, witnesses, or reviewers, or to misuse the appeal process for harassment, may themselves be addressed under this policy.

9. Responsibilities of Maintainers

Maintainers and administrators are expected to:

* Apply these standards consistently and impartially.
* Respond to reports appropriately, taking severity and available resources into account.
* Protect sensitive reporting information and restrict access where practical.
* Identify and manage conflicts of interest when assessing reports.
* Take reasonable steps to prevent retaliation.
* Use project privileges only for legitimate project purposes.
* Explain moderation decisions when appropriate and safe.
* Review this policy periodically and update it as the project evolves.

Maintainers are not required to disclose private investigation details or guarantee a particular outcome.

Reports must not be ignored solely because the person involved is a contributor, reviewer, administrator, or maintainer.

10. Good-Faith Reporting and Anti-Retaliation

MedOrbits encourages good-faith reporting of misconduct and cooperation with reasonable reviews.

No participant should be threatened, harassed, punished, or unfairly excluded for making a good-faith report, providing relevant evidence, requesting assistance, or participating in an investigation.

Knowingly fabricated reports or deliberate misuse of the reporting process may themselves be addressed under this policy.

An unsubstantiated report does not, by itself, establish dishonesty or misconduct.

11. Relationship to Other Project Policies

This Code of Conduct operates alongside the project’s other policies and documentation:

* CONTRIBUTING.md — contribution requirements and development workflows.
* SECURITY.md — security vulnerability reporting and disclosure procedures.
* LICENSE — applicable terms governing use and distribution of project materials.
* README.md — project information, setup instructions, and published contact channels.

Where another policy establishes a more specific procedure for a particular matter, participants should follow that procedure in addition to the behavioral expectations described here.

This Code of Conduct does not replace applicable law, contractual obligations, or formal security and privacy requirements.

12. Policy Updates

MedOrbits may revise this Code of Conduct as its community, technology, and operational needs evolve.

Material changes should be documented through the project’s normal version-control and review processes.

Participants are expected to follow the currently published policy when engaging in covered project spaces.

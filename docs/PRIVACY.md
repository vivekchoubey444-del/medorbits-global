1. Purpose

This document establishes the privacy principles and data-protection requirements for MedOrbits, an education and consultancy platform that may provide course discovery, medical admission guidance, official-document resources, application management, document uploads, support services, career information, and related digital services.

It is intended to guide:

* Website and API development.
* Collection and processing of personal information.
* Account and application management.
* Handling of academic records and identity documents.
* Use of analytics, cookies, AI services, and third-party providers.
* Data retention, deletion, and access control.
* Privacy requests and security incident response.

Implementation requirement: This document is a privacy-policy and engineering reference. It does not establish that MedOrbits currently uses every service or processes every data category listed below. Actual processing must be documented accurately and reflected in the public-facing privacy notice.

2. Privacy principles

MedOrbits should follow these principles:

1. Purpose limitation: Collect information for specified, legitimate service purposes.
2. Data minimisation: Request only the information necessary for a feature or transaction.
3. Transparency: Explain what information is collected, why it is used, and when it is shared.
4. Security: Protect personal information against unauthorised access, alteration, disclosure, or loss.
5. Limited retention: Keep personal information only for as long as required for the documented purpose or applicable obligations.
6. Access control: Restrict access to staff and service providers who need the information.
7. Accuracy: Provide a way to correct inaccurate account and application information.
8. Accountability: Maintain appropriate records of processing, access, and security-related actions.
9. Privacy by design: Consider privacy before introducing new features or third-party integrations.
10. No misleading assurances: Never claim that information is anonymous, encrypted, deleted, or never shared unless the actual implementation supports that statement.

3. Scope

This policy applies to personal information processed through:

* The MedOrbits website and mobile-accessible pages.
* Registration, login, and account-management features.
* Application and consultancy forms.
* Academic and identity-document uploads.
* Support tickets and communications.
* Payment and refund workflows, if enabled.
* Notifications and service emails.
* Analytics and diagnostic tools, if enabled.
* AI-powered features, if enabled.
* Administrative dashboards and backend APIs.
* Hosting, storage, and other service providers processing data for MedOrbits.

It covers information collected directly from users and information generated by the platform, such as application status, support history, and security logs.

4. Categories of information

The platform should document which categories it actually collects.

4.1 Account information

Potential fields include:

* Name or display name.
* Email address.
* Phone number.
* Account identifier.
* Authentication and account-status metadata.
* Language and communication preferences.

Passwords must never be stored in plaintext.

4.2 Education and application information

Depending on the service requested, this may include:

* Courses and institutions of interest.
* Examination and admission-cycle information.
* Academic qualifications and marks.
* Application details and status.
* Preferred locations or programmes.
* Documents required for a specific application.

Do not request complete academic records or identity details if the service can be delivered without them.

4.3 Uploaded documents

If document uploads are supported, users may provide:

* Academic certificates and marksheets.
* Identity documents.
* Application forms.
* Eligibility or supporting records.
* Other documents specifically required for a requested service.

These documents may contain sensitive personal information. Restrict collection, access, sharing, and retention accordingly.

4.4 Support communications

Information may include:

* Ticket subject and message.
* Contact details supplied by the user.
* Relevant transaction or application references.
* Attachments submitted for troubleshooting.
* Responses and support history.

Ask users not to include passwords, authentication codes, complete payment credentials, or unrelated sensitive information in support messages.

4.5 Payment information

If payments are enabled, MedOrbits may process payment-related metadata such as:

* Order and transaction identifiers.
* Amount and currency.
* Payment status.
* Refund request and reconciliation details.
* Provider-generated references.

Payment card details and security codes should be handled by the authorised payment provider rather than stored directly by MedOrbits.

4.6 Technical information

Depending on hosting and configuration, the platform may process:

* IP address.
* Browser and device information.
* Requested URLs and timestamps.
* Error reports and diagnostic logs.
* Session and security metadata.
* Cookie or consent preferences.

Collect technical data only to the extent necessary for security, operation, analytics, or another disclosed purpose.

5. Purposes of processing

Personal information may be used for the following purposes when applicable and appropriately authorised:

Purpose	Examples
Account administration	Registration, login, account recovery
Education services	Course discovery and personalised guidance
Application management	Processing, tracking, and updating applications
Document handling	Receiving, reviewing, and returning requested documents
Customer support	Investigating tickets and responding to requests
Payments	Order processing, reconciliation, and refunds
Service communications	Essential confirmations and requested updates
Security	Preventing abuse, investigating incidents, and protecting accounts
Platform improvement	Diagnosing errors and improving usability
Legal compliance	Meeting applicable legal obligations
AI-assisted features	Providing a specifically requested AI-enabled service, subject to applicable safeguards

Do not reuse personal information for unrelated marketing, profiling, or disclosure purposes without an appropriate legal basis and required notice or consent.

6. Legal basis and user choice

The applicable legal basis depends on the processing activity, the jurisdiction, and the service being provided.

Before collecting information, identify the relevant basis under applicable law, such as valid consent, a permitted legitimate or authorised purpose, contractual necessity, or a legal obligation where applicable.

Where consent is required:

* Explain the purpose clearly.
* Obtain consent through an appropriate mechanism.
* Do not use preselected consent controls as a substitute for meaningful consent.
* Record consent and withdrawal where necessary.
* Provide a reasonable way to withdraw consent.
* Stop processing that relies on withdrawn consent unless another lawful basis applies.

A user should not be required to consent to unrelated optional analytics or marketing merely to access a service that does not need those activities.

7. Data sharing and third-party processors

MedOrbits should share personal information only where necessary, authorised, and consistent with its disclosed purposes and applicable law.

Potential recipients may include:

* Hosting and infrastructure providers.
* Database and file-storage providers.
* Email or communication services.
* Payment processors.
* AI service providers, where enabled.
* Security and diagnostic providers.
* Authorised consultants or support staff.
* Government authorities or other parties where legally required or otherwise lawfully permitted.

The platform should maintain an accurate inventory of providers that receive or process personal information.

Third-party safeguards

Where appropriate:

* Assess the provider’s security and privacy practices.
* Establish suitable contractual data-protection terms.
* Limit the information sent to the provider.
* Restrict provider access to what is necessary.
* Review data-retention and deletion capabilities.
* Evaluate cross-border processing and applicable legal requirements.
* Avoid transmitting academic or identity documents to third parties unless necessary and authorised.

Do not claim that data is never shared if hosting, analytics, payment, AI, or other processors receive it.

8. AI services and automated processing

If MedOrbits provides AI-assisted guidance, summaries, search, or document processing, the following controls apply.

* Disclose when an AI service materially processes user-provided information.
* Keep provider credentials on the server.
* Minimise personal data included in prompts or requests.
* Avoid transmitting identity documents or complete academic records unless necessary and appropriately authorised.
* Review provider retention, training, and data-use settings.
* Do not assume that provider data is excluded from training without checking the applicable terms and configuration.
* Treat generated responses as potentially inaccurate.
* Provide appropriate human review for consequential admission or application decisions.
* Do not use AI alone to make consequential eligibility, financial, or legal determinations without suitable safeguards and a lawful basis.

AI-generated guidance must not be represented as an official decision from a regulator, examination authority, university, or government department.

9. Cookies and similar technologies

The platform should maintain an accurate inventory of cookies, local storage, session storage, pixels, and other browser-side technologies.

Potential categories include:

* Essential: Authentication, session integrity, security, and core functionality.
* Preferences: Language and accessibility settings.
* Analytics: Usage and performance measurement, if enabled.
* Marketing: Advertising or campaign measurement, if enabled.

Use only the categories required by the actual implementation.

Where applicable law requires consent for non-essential technologies, obtain consent before activating them and provide a usable way to change or withdraw that choice.

Do not describe a cookie as essential merely to avoid consent requirements. Avoid storing passwords, identity documents, or other highly sensitive information in browser storage.

10. Data retention

MedOrbits should define retention periods by data category and purpose.

Data category	Retention principle
Account data	Retain while needed to provide the account and handle applicable obligations
Application records	Retain for the documented application lifecycle and applicable requirements
Uploaded documents	Retain only as long as needed for the requested service or a documented obligation
Support tickets	Retain for support, dispute handling, and applicable requirements
Payment records	Retain according to accounting, reconciliation, fraud-prevention, and legal requirements
Security logs	Retain for a defined security and operational period
Analytics data	Retain according to its disclosed purpose and configuration
Consent records	Retain as needed to demonstrate and administer consent
Backups	Retain according to a documented backup lifecycle

These are principles, not fixed retention periods. Establish actual periods based on the service, applicable law, provider capabilities, and business requirements.

When the purpose expires, delete or anonymise the information where appropriate and legally permissible. Account deletion does not necessarily mean immediate removal of every record from backups or records that must lawfully be retained.

11. User rights and privacy requests

Subject to applicable law, users may be entitled to request access to, correction of, or deletion of their personal information, withdraw consent, raise grievances, or exercise other relevant rights.

MedOrbits should provide a clear privacy contact and an accessible request process.

Request-handling procedure

1. Receive the request through the published channel.
2. Acknowledge it where appropriate.
3. Verify the requester’s identity proportionately.
4. Identify the relevant systems and records.
5. Assess applicable legal obligations, exceptions, and retention requirements.
6. Complete the request within the applicable timeframe.
7. Record the outcome securely.
8. Communicate the result or explain a lawful limitation.

Do not require users to email identity documents unnecessarily just to make a basic privacy enquiry.

Deletion requests

Where deletion is applicable:

* Remove or anonymise the relevant records from active systems.
* Revoke active sessions and access where appropriate.
* Remove or invalidate access to private files.
* Propagate deletion to relevant processors where required and feasible.
* Apply the documented backup expiry process.
* Retain only records that must or may lawfully be retained.

Do not promise immediate deletion from every backup or third-party system unless the actual process supports that promise.

12. Security measures

MedOrbits should implement technical and organisational safeguards appropriate to the risks of the data processed.

Recommended controls include:

* HTTPS for production traffic.
* Password hashing using an appropriate modern algorithm.
* Secure session management and token handling.
* Multi-factor authentication for privileged access where appropriate.
* Server-side validation and parameterised database queries.
* Role-based and resource-level authorization.
* Private storage for uploaded documents.
* Restricted and auditable administrative access.
* Rate limiting and abuse prevention.
* Secret management outside source control.
* Dependency updates and security reviews.
* Secure backup storage and tested recovery procedures.
* Log redaction and restricted log access.
* Incident detection and response procedures.

Security controls must be verified in the actual implementation. Do not claim end-to-end encryption, independent certification, or other specific security guarantees unless they have been established.

13. Student and minor information

Educational services may be used by minors. Before collecting information from children or processing information in a way that triggers child-specific obligations, assess the applicable law and requirements for the relevant service and jurisdiction.

Where required:

* Implement appropriate age-related safeguards.
* Obtain verifiable parental or guardian consent when legally required.
* Provide clear, age-appropriate privacy explanations.
* Avoid unnecessary profiling, tracking, or targeted advertising directed at children.
* Restrict access to children’s personal information.
* Avoid collecting sensitive documents before they are needed.

Do not assume that all users are adults merely because they are applying to higher education.

14. International transfers

If personal information is stored or processed outside India, assess the applicable cross-border transfer rules and any restrictions or contractual obligations.

Document:

* Which providers process the data.
* The relevant processing locations, where known.
* Applicable transfer safeguards.
* Provider retention and deletion practices.
* Access controls for overseas processing.

Do not claim that information remains exclusively in India unless all relevant infrastructure and provider arrangements support that statement.

15. Data accuracy and official information

MedOrbits may publish official-document links, examination notices, admission guidance, fees, scholarship information, and institutional details.

These public resources should be distinguished from personal data processing.

For information accuracy:

* Prefer the responsible primary authority.
* Record source URLs and verification dates.
* Label historical or estimated information clearly.
* Correct outdated or inaccurate information promptly.
* Do not fabricate official documents, approvals, notices, or admission outcomes.

See docs/DATA_VERIFICATION.md for the data verification process.

16. Security incidents and data breaches

A suspected privacy or security incident must be assessed promptly.

Response procedure

1. Detect and report the incident through the designated internal channel.
2. Contain unauthorised access or ongoing exposure.
3. Preserve relevant evidence and security logs.
4. Assess the affected systems, data categories, and potential impact.
5. Notify responsible personnel and providers as appropriate.
6. Determine applicable legal and contractual notification obligations.
7. Notify affected individuals and authorities when required.
8. Restore secure operation and verify recovery.
9. Document the incident, corrective actions, and lessons learned.

Do not delay containment while waiting for a complete investigation.

Maintain an incident-response plan that identifies responsible roles, contact procedures, evidence handling, and escalation paths.

17. Privacy by design

Before adding a new feature, review:

* What personal information it needs.
* Why the information is necessary.
* Whether a less intrusive approach would work.
* Who can access the information.
* Whether third parties receive it.
* How long it must be retained.
* How users can exercise their rights.
* Whether the feature introduces additional risks for minors or vulnerable users.
* How deletion, correction, and security incidents will be handled.

For high-risk processing, conduct an appropriate privacy impact assessment before release.

18. Children’s data, marketing, and communications

Service-related communications should be distinguished from optional promotional communications.

* Send essential service messages only where necessary and appropriate.
* Obtain required consent for marketing communications.
* Provide a usable unsubscribe or preference mechanism where applicable.
* Honour opt-out requests within the applicable requirements.
* Avoid using private academic or identity information for unrelated advertising.
* Apply additional safeguards when the user is a child.

Do not use application submissions or document uploads as automatic consent for unrelated promotional campaigns.

19. Public-facing privacy notice

The public-facing privacy notice should be accessible from the website footer, account creation screens, application forms, document-upload forms, and other relevant collection points.

It should accurately explain:

1. Who operates MedOrbits and how to contact the responsible entity.
2. What personal information is collected.
3. Why each category is processed.
4. The applicable legal basis or consent mechanism, where relevant.
5. Which service providers receive information.
6. Whether information is processed internationally.
7. Retention periods or the criteria used to determine them.
8. User rights and how to exercise them.
9. Cookie and tracking practices.
10. Security and incident-handling practices.
11. How policy changes will be communicated.
12. The process for privacy complaints and grievances.

A repository policy document is not a substitute for publishing an appropriate, legally reviewed privacy notice to users.

20. Privacy contact and grievance handling

Publish a working contact channel for privacy enquiries and requests.

Configure the following placeholders with real, monitored contact details before publication:

* Privacy contact: [Insert verified privacy contact email]
* Grievance contact: [Insert designated grievance contact, if applicable]
* Postal address: [Insert verified business address, if required]

Do not publish placeholder contact details as though they were operational. Do not invent a privacy officer, grievance officer, business address, or email address.

Requests should be tracked securely, with access limited to personnel responsible for handling them.

21. Compliance review

Before launching or materially changing a service, assess applicable privacy and data-protection requirements, including relevant Indian law and rules in force at the time of processing.

Where relevant, review the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, applicable rules and commencement notifications, the Information Technology Act, 2000, associated rules, and any other applicable sector-specific or international requirements.

Do not assume that every provision has commenced or applies identically to every processing activity. Obtain qualified legal advice for jurisdiction-specific obligations, children’s data, consent, cross-border processing, retention, and breach notification.
